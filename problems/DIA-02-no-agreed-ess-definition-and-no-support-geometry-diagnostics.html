No agreed ESS definition and no support-geometry diagnostics

Diagnostics & empirical validation
Very high priority
Partially addressed
A global effective sample size is a scalar function of the weight distribution, so it does not encode where in covariate space the support lies or whether the supported region is relevant to the decision contrast. Statement

A global effective sample size is a scalar function of the weight distribution, so it does not encode where in covariate space the support lies or whether the supported region is relevant to the decision contrast. Reporting is demonstrably incomplete in practice: of 117 oncology MAICs reviewed by Farinasso et al. 2025 (doi:10.1016/j.vhri.2025.101088), only three fulfilled all NICE recommendations and the distribution of weights was among the least reported items. The claim that software reports ESS alone and never computes contrast-specific influence is too strong: cpaic, an unreleased development package on GitHub that is not on CRAN, already exports a weight panel with effective sample size, entropy efficiency, weight variation, concentration and residual balance, plus an edge influence function for a named treatment contrast. What remains missing is a definition set agreed across packages and the support-geometry diagnostics that no package computes: region-specific ESS, convex-hull distance, density-ratio and optimal-transport cost, out-of-distribution maps, and balance on omitted moments.

Why it is open

Effective sample size is the squared sum of the weights divided by the sum of their squares, so it is invariant to where the weights sit in covariate space; the same value can come from a broad shifted distribution or from a decision-relevant subgroup with no source support at all. Balance is conventionally reported on the moments that were matched, which is tautological, while balance on the omitted moments is the informative check and is usually skipped. Because no body owns the definitions and at least three competing ESS calculations for population adjustment have been proposed, two analyses of the same evidence can report incomparable numbers, and no empirical knowledge accumulates about which diagnostic values predict bias.

Probable solution or research direction

Standardize and extend what exists rather than specify a panel from scratch: agree one definition set across packages, starting from the multi-metric weight panel and contrast-specific influence already implemented in cpaic, and add the support-geometry diagnostics no package computes, namely region-specific ESS, convex-hull distance between source and target support, density-ratio and optimal-transport cost, out-of-distribution maps, and balance on omitted as well as fitted moments. Require the agreed panel in publications and submissions. Evaluate each member of the panel in simulation for its ability to predict realized error, and drop the ones that only describe the fit.

Verdict. Partially addressed. Auditors disagreed and the disagreement was evidence-bearing on both sides. Reopened by the full-text reading: 1 finding from 1 paper survived both the reader and an independent second reviewer, so the verdict moves from unverifiable to partially-addressed, on a single finding.

The source titled this. “ESS reported alone and overlap diagnostics are not standardized” The title says software reports effective sample size alone, which the audit judged too strong given an implemented multi-metric weight panel and contrast-specific edge influence.

The source said. “Overlap is summarized by a single effective sample size, which cannot show the geometry of covariate support, where the analysis is extrapolating, or whether the supported region is relevant to the target contrast. The definitions and displays of overlap, ESS, extrapolation and modifier-balance diagnostics also differ between packages and publications, so two analyses of the same evidence report incomparable numbers.”

What was wrong with it. The claim was unattributed in the source and is now sourced: the literature auditor supplied Farinasso et al. 2025 (doi:10.1016/j.vhri.2025.101088) for heterogeneous and incomplete diagnostic reporting, and a 2024 paper proposing three competing ESS calculations (doi:10.1186/s12874-024-02412-1) for the non-standardization of ESS itself. GPT-5.6 Sol read cpaic at commit 9d150e9 and found a multi-metric weight panel and contrast-specific edge influence already implemented, so the ESS-only claim fails there; the two auditors disagreed on whether the entry as written was supported or overstated, and the weaker reading is published here.

Dissent. literature dissented (open): This entry is source_unsourced; I found real references that support it. Farinasso et al. 2025 scoping-reviewed 117 oncology MAICs and reports that only 3 fulfilled all NICE DSU recommendations and that among the least reported items was the distribution of weights, with mean sample-size reduction 44.9%. That is direct published evidence that diagnostic reporting practice is heterogeneous and suboptimal. Independently, a 2024 BMC Med Res Methodol paper proposes three new ESS calculations for population adjustment, which is itself evidence that no single ESS definition is fixed across packages and publications. I found no PAIC package or publication reporting local (region-specific) ESS, optimal-transport cost, convex-hull distance, out-of-distribution maps, balance on omitted moments, or influence alignment between IPD study edges and the decision contrast; those remain absent, though absence found by search is weaker evidence than the reporting review. codex dissented (overstated): C4 through C7 follow directly from ESS = (sum w)^2 / sum(w^2): the same weights can be reassigned to different covariate locations without changing ESS, despite different local support and contrast relevance. The claim that software reports only ESS and never computes contrast-specific influence is already false for cpaic at the requested commit. It exports weight_diagnostics() with ESS, entropy efficiency, weight variation, concentration, and residual balance, plus edge_influence() for a named treatment contrast. The latter is documented as approximate for multi-arm networks. Local ESS, convex-hull distance, density ratios, and transport costs are complementary diagnostics requiring careful definitions and validation, not established replacements for ESS. Cross-package standardization and fuller support-geometry diagnostics remain open.

How the evidence falls in time

8 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2012 to 2024. Partial progress is on the record, and later work still calls this open. Both belong in view.

This problem is recurrent: three or more papers spanning at least eight years assert it independently. Sustained restatement by authors who mostly do not cite each other is the strongest evidence this reading can offer that a gap is real rather than one group’s framing.
