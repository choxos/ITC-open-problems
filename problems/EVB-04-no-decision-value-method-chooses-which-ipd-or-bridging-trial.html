Statement

Methods for choosing the next data collection by expected decision value exist and are mature: conditional trial design sizes and configures a new trial against an existing network to reach a target power or precision rather than by expected decision value to reach a target power or precision rather than by expected decision value to reach a target power or precision rather than by expected decision value to reach a target power or precision rather than by expected decision value to reach a target power or precision rather than by expected decision value to reach a target power or precision rather than by expected decision value to reach a target power or precision rather than by expected decision value (Salanti et al. 2018, doi:10.1186/s13063-018-2740-2; Nikolakopoulou et al. 2014, pmid:25225031), and expected value of sample information has practical tutorials and worked prioritization examples (doi:10.1177/0272989X211026292; doi:10.1136/bmjopen-2024-092158). Neither has been applied to deciding which individual patient dataset to acquire for a population-adjusted comparison, nor to sizing and allocating a bridging trial that would connect two disconnected subnetworks. Whether acquisition in ITC practice is in fact opportunistic is undocumented: no auditor found a survey of it.

Why it is open

The general machinery is decision-theoretic and only produces an answer once the decision, utility, costs, access probability, model uncertainty and target population are specified; for a population-adjusted comparison none of these is currently packaged as an input to acquisition. The value of an additional IPD study depends on the target population, the bridge in use and the decision threshold, and no published formulation ties those to a request for a specific dataset. For disconnected networks the harder version, choosing which bridge treatment to run and how to allocate sample across the gap, was not found by any auditor. What is open is the ITC-specific operational formulation and its robustness, not the existence of a principled criterion.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
Salanti et al. 2018, Trials Four-step framework identifying the optimal design for a new trial that updates an existing network while minimizing required sample size, allowing the new trial to omit some interventions and still contribute direct and indirect evidence.
Nikolakopoulou et al. 2014, Biometrical Journal Derives conditional power of a network meta-analysis to inform the design of future trials; earliest of the conditional trial design papers and establishes that sample size can be computed conditional on the existing network.
Heath et al. 2022, Medical Decision Making Practical guidance and recommendations for four model-based methods of computing expected value of sample information efficiently; shows the machinery for deciding what data to collect is mature and tutorialized.
Glynn et al. 2025, BMJ Open (DiaDeM) Worked example of value-of-information analysis used to prioritize which data to collect, showing the acquisition-as-decision-object framing is present in the wider literature.

Probable solution or research direction

Adapt the existing value-of-information and conditional trial design machinery to the ITC setting rather than build new criteria: specify the decision, utility, costs, access probability and target population, then rank candidate IPD acquisitions by expected decision value. Extend conditional trial design from adding a trial to a connected network to the disconnected case, where the objects of choice are the bridge treatment, the sample size and the allocation across the gap. Establishing whether current acquisition is opportunistic requires a survey of practice, which no auditor could find, so that claim should be tested before it is used to justify the programme.

GPT-5.6 Sol, technical and source-code lens
open / overstated. C2 and C3 are technically valid research directions: expected-utility or value-of-information criteria can rank candidate IPD acquisitions, and optimal-design criteria can choose bridge treatments, sample size, and allocation. The central empirical claim C1, that current decisions are generally opportunistic, is unsupported here. The literal statement that no formal object exists is false because decision theory already supplies such objects once the decision, utility, costs, predictive model, access probability, and relevant uncertainties are specified. What remains open is an ITC-specific operational formulation and its robustness, not the existence of a principled criterion. This is generally a roadmap and resource-allocation issue rather than a universal threat to identification or decision-grade validity.
Grok 4.5, recency lens
open / supported. Search assumed VOI and optimal design had already made IPD acquisition decision-driven. General value-of-information methods for HTA research prioritization and study design are mature (ISPOR VOI task force reports; Jackson et al. reviews of VOI in policy models; EVPI/EVPPI/EVSI tooling). That infrastructure means C2 is a defensible direction rather than science fiction. However, no retrieved 2020–2026 paper or software release implements a standard, decision-linked criterion for which IPD dataset to request for PAIC/ITC, or optimal sample allocation of a bridging trial across a gap between disconnected subnetworks, as a routine acquisition object. Dose-response and other NMA extensions discuss reconnecting disconnected networks when common agents exist; that is model-based reconnection, not VOI-guided bridge-trial design. No CRAN/GitHub signal for multinma/maicplus/netmeta/outstandR closed this acquisition gap. C1 (opportunistic access in practice) remains descriptive and unfalsified by a competing formal acquisition framework. Queries: VOI/EVSI IPD meta-analysis; VOI network meta research prioritization; optimal bridge trial disconnected NMA; active IPD acquisition strategy. Vote open with moderate-low confidence because VOI is adjacent and may be applied without being published as ITC-specific method.

How the evidence falls in time

7 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2016 to 2023. The most recent paper to touch this reports progress on it, and every paper calling it open is older.

It is also being closed in pieces: partial results come from more than one paper in more than one year, each covering a different part.

Does the later work answer the earlier work? Put to an independent reviewer with the older and newer claims side by side, it judged that the later work answers a component of it, leaving a named part (high confidence). The 2023 disconnected-network paper gives a substantive, network-dependent rule for selecting the treatment pair in a new bridging trial. It fixes equal allocation and does not formulate acquisition or trial design by decision value, costs, or utility, so it resolves only one component of the earlier gap. What remains: Choosing which IPD to acquire; decision-value or cost-based selection and sample allocation for a bridging trial; and evidence on whether such methods are used in practice.
