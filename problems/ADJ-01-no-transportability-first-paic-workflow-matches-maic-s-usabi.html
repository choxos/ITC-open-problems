No transportability-first PAIC workflow matches MAIC’s usability

Adjacent-field methods
Very high priority
Overstated
PAIC has already been restated in transportability and estimand terms: Chandler and Ishak (arXiv:2602.17041) reframe PAICs as transportability problems and show that pairwise PAIC identifies an effect defined in the comparator population, so applying it elsewhere is an additional transport step, and Remiro-Azocar (arXiv:2210.01757) treats the transportability of marginal versus conditional estimands in evidence synthesis. Statement

PAIC has already been restated in transportability and estimand terms: Chandler and Ishak (arXiv:2602.17041) reframe PAICs as transportability problems and show that pairwise PAIC identifies an effect defined in the comparator population, so applying it elsewhere is an additional transport step, and Remiro-Azocar (arXiv:2210.01757) treats the transportability of marginal versus conditional estimands in evidence synthesis. The estimators the source calls underused also exist for PAIC: Park et al. (doi:10.1016/j.jval.2023.11.011) implement and compare inverse odds weighting, regression adjustment and a doubly robust method for unanchored time-to-event comparisons, Campbell and Remiro-Azocar (arXiv:2505.00113) give augmented doubly robust MAIC, and drMAIC 0.1.0 ships it. What has not been done is the usability half: no transportability-first workflow, guidance document or reporting template matches MAIC’s one-page explanation, and routine HTA submissions still do not use these estimators. One auditor read that remainder as a workflow and communication objective rather than a well-posed statistical open problem.

Why it is open

The recasting exists on paper but nothing carries it into practice. The transport framing requires nuisance models, an explicit target covariate distribution and a diagnostic suite that MAIC’s single weighting step conceals, so the explainable option keeps winning over the more rigorous one. No guidance, training material or reporting template has been rebuilt around the transport vocabulary, so a submission written in transport terms costs the analyst more and buys no procedural credit. The case where the framing matters most, a target described only by published summaries, is also the case where standard transport estimators do not apply directly, so the umbrella cannot simply be imported.

Probable solution or research direction

The derivation work is done, so the direction is dissemination and tooling rather than a new framework: convert the published estimand-based recasting into a workflow, checklist and reporting template whose inputs, outputs and one-page explanation are as simple as MAIC’s. Validate the doubly robust and inverse-odds estimators that already exist for PAIC in realistic HTA settings and get them into guidance, so the causally principled option becomes the operationally convenient one. Measure success by whether a committee can follow the analysis, not by whether the estimator is new.

Verification

Verdict. Overstated. 2 independent auditors judged the claim as stated too strong. PAIC has now been formally restated in transportability/estimand terms in the methods literature, but that restatement has not been converted into a workflow, guidance and reporting template with MAIC’s operational simplicity, and routine HTA submissions still do not use inverse-odds, standardisation or doubly robust estimators.

The source titled this. “Recasting PAIC as a transportability problem without losing usability” The title poses the transportability recasting as the open work, but the audit found it already published (arXiv:2602.17041, arXiv:2210.01757), leaving only the usability half.

The source said. “Population-adjusted indirect comparison is a restricted case of the causal transportability problem, yet it is practised as a set of bespoke HTA estimators with their own vocabulary. The open question is whether PAIC can be rebuilt on an explicit source/target transport framework while keeping the practical accessibility that made MAIC attractive.”

What was wrong with it. The source cited nothing and claimed both that the recasting was undone and that PAIC underuses inverse-odds weighting, standardization and doubly robust estimators; the literature auditor found the recasting published (arXiv:2602.17041, arXiv:2210.01757) and all three estimators implemented for PAIC (doi:10.1016/j.jval.2023.11.011, arXiv:2505.00113, drMAIC 0.1.0). The claim was unattributed in the source and is now sourced; what survives is the usability half, which GPT-5.6 Sol read as a workflow objective rather than a statistical problem.

Dissent. grok dissented (supported-with-caveat): Inverted-prior search finds a near-literal hit: Chandler and Ishak (arXiv:2602.17041, 2026) reframe PAICs as transportability problems from an estimand-based HTA perspective, distinguish conditional versus marginal transport, and clarify when SEMA alone fails for non-collapsible measures. That paper formalizes and diagnoses the dual-transport structure of pairwise MAIC/STC; it does not ship a transportability-first workflow with MAIC-like one-page accessibility, software, training, or reporting templates. Separately, Campbell and Remiro-Azócar (arXiv:2505.00113, 2025) import doubly robust augmentation into entropy-balancing MAIC for unanchored and external-control settings, so C4 is already partly outdated for pairwise unanchored work. C2 is supported as a restricted-case relationship; C3 remains broadly true of HTA practice; C6 is only partly true because target-AgD transport methods exist but still struggle where only published summaries are available; C8 and C9 remain largely open on the usability half of the dual requirement.
