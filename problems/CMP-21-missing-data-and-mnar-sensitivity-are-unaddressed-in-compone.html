Statement

No missing-data framework has been made congenial with component transport. Multiple imputation and MNAR tipping-point sensitivity have already been applied in an ordinary MAIC: Esnault et al. 2025 imputed missing covariates inside a prespecified propensity-score workflow with roughly half of ECOG performance status missing, and probed missingness with quantitative bias analysis including an explicit tipping-point analysis. What is absent is any specification of how an imputation model should be made compatible with a likelihood that integrates over a target covariate distribution and shares component parameters across subnetworks, and of how a single MNAR sensitivity parameter propagates simultaneously through the adjustment step, the covariate overlap, and the bridging assumption. Implemented component ML-NMR rejects missing effect modifiers and outcomes and states that it does not impute.

Why it is open

In PAIC, dropping incomplete records redefines the population being reweighted or standardized, so transport assumptions are evaluated against a population that is not the one reported, and the overlap diagnostics are computed on the wrong set. An imputation model must be congenial with a likelihood that integrates over a target covariate distribution and shares component parameters across subnetworks, and no one has written down what congeniality means in that setting. Inverse probability of censoring weighting also interacts multiplicatively with MAIC weights, compounding variance and positivity problems, and a single MNAR sensitivity parameter propagates through several layers at once with no framework saying how those effects combine.

Probable solution or research direction

Adapt the existing MAIC-level multiple imputation and tipping-point workflow rather than inventing one, and state the congeniality conditions between the imputation model and a component transport likelihood that integrates over a target distribution. Add inverse probability of censoring weighting for outcome missingness with explicit treatment of its interaction with adjustment weights, and let imputation uncertainty flow into the standardized target estimand rather than being handled outside the model. Specify MNAR sensitivity parameters that are interpretable for both outcome and effect-modifier missingness and report how they propagate jointly with the population-adjustment and bridging assumptions.
