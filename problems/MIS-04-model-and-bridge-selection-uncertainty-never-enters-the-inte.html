Model and bridge selection uncertainty never enters the interval

Missing data & uncertainty propagation
High priority
Partially addressed
When the same data select a PAIC model or bridge and ordinary conditional intervals are then reported, nominal coverage is generally not guaranteed, although the distortion can run in either direction and a prespecified or externally chosen model does not create the problem at all. Model averaging as the response is established in evidence synthesis generally (Rover, Wandel and Friede 2019; Bartos et al., arXiv:2110.01076) but has not been applied to PAIC, and predictive stacking needs a held-out unit aligned with the prediction target and dependence structure, so patient, study, and contrast level prediction are different estimands to choose between rather than an ambiguity that blocks the method. For bridges the difficulty is narrower than the source claimed: likelihood-based weights can be formed when candidate bridges imply different observed-data likelihoods or predictions, and are unavailable only for observationally equivalent identifying assumptions, which need prior elicitation or sensitivity analysis instead. No PAIC software feeds model or bridge averaging into the reported effect interval; in cpaic, an unreleased development package on GitHub that is not on CRAN, loo() is observation-level and warns that it does not validate cross-gap prediction, while prior_sensitivity() and bridge_fragility() remain diagnostics. The same failure occurs one step earlier, at the treatment partition. Whether doses, formulations, schedules, devices, class members or control conditions become one node or two is settled before any model is fitted, on clinical judgment or on a deviance comparison among candidate groupings, and the alternatives that fit almost as well are neither reported nor propagated into the interval or the ranking. Model fit cannot arbitrate that choice, because candidate partitions are not nested in a way that makes deviance comparable across different data configurations and because splitting can disconnect the network, which is a structural rather than a statistical objection. The same defect appears wherever a study’s contribution is reconstructed from an assumed nuisance parameter rather than reported: most published crossover trials give no precision estimate that respects the paired design, so a meta-analyst must impute a within-participant correlation, use first-period data only, or exclude the trial, and whichever is chosen is then conditioned on as known. Across 83 crossover trials assembled for one network, 19 (23 percent) reported a paired precision estimate and 50 (60 percent) supplied a point estimate and precision usable without further assumptions, falling to 26 (31 percent) once a washout period was also required (doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0133023), so both the eligible set and the imputed correlation move the contributed weights, and no study has reported how far pooled effects or rankings shift between the resulting eligible sets.

Why it is open

Selection happens on the same data used for estimation, so the reported interval is conditional on a choice it does not price. The standard remedy needs a predictive unit at which the stacking weights are meaningful, and in a network the choice among patient, study, and contrast has to be made deliberately as a statement of the prediction target rather than inherited from a default. Projection predictive selection controls predictive loss and does not by itself deliver valid post-selection intervals, and robust Bayes covers only the model and prior classes actually represented, so neither is a finished answer and neither has been tried in PAIC. For bridges that are observationally equivalent no fit statistic can separate them, and the only honest input is a prior on assumption plausibility, which nobody has elicited.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
Rover, Wandel and Friede 2019, Statistics in Medicine (locator not supplied by the auditor) heavy-tailed mixture priors for effect estimation in meta-analysis, which effectively result in a model-averaging technique robust to prior-data conflict; established for meta-analysis, not applied to population-adjusted comparisons
Bartos et al., Bayesian Model-Averaged Meta-Analysis in Medicine Bayesian model averaging over meta-analytic models so that model uncertainty enters the reported estimate; again for meta-analysis rather than for population-adjusted or network settings
cpaic, unreleased development package on GitHub (not on CRAN), commit 9d150e9 provides prior_sensitivity() and bridge_fragility() diagnostics and an observation-level loo() that warns it does not validate cross-gap prediction; no model or bridge averaging enters the reported effect interval
Efthimiou et al. 2016, Research Synthesis Methods (GetReal review) States that no recipe exists for deciding whether similar-but-not-identical interventions become one node or two, that the choice is left to clinical judgment plus goodness-of-fit comparison between candidate structures, and that as included studies adopt increasingly different node definitions transitivity becomes less plausible and inconsistency or heterogeneity rises.
Welton et al. 2015, Medical Decision Making Compares five treatment-definition models for one dataset and finds the deviance information criterion spread narrow, 168.6 to 180.5, while the resulting decision changes; resolves the choice by expert judgment and states that the approach can help determine whether grouping is reasonable only with clinical input. The closest thing to a fit-based arbitration of node granularity, and it shows fit does not arbitrate.
Mills et al. 2013, BMJ Survey of the effects of excluding treatments from network meta-analyses, reporting node-level effect changes up to 3.64-fold and reordering of the top three treatments in half of 18 networks. Explicitly declines to question the clustering of regimens into nodes by the original analysts and names that clustering as an additional, unquantified source of variability.
Ishaq et al. 2023, European Spine Journal Delphi consensus study built specifically to group conservative neck-pain treatments into network nodes, which records that no generally recommended procedure exists for grouping similar-enough treatments into nodes and that several different methods could therefore be followed.
Zhang et al. 2026, Frontiers in Medicine Prespecifies three node layers, 17, 5 and 4 nodes, for a single sedation dataset and reports that the numerically best treatment in the finest layer was a sparse variant supported only by indirect evidence, with interventions at different conceptual levels risking inflated treatment hierarchies.
Ades et al. 2024, Research Synthesis Methods Reviews twenty years of network meta-analysis controversies and identifies lumping over doses and co-treatments as an avoidable source of quantitative heterogeneity, so the concern is not confined to one clinical area.
Li, Yu, Hawkins and Dickersin 2015, Design, Analysis, and Reporting of Crossover Trials for Inclusion in a Meta-Analysis, PLOS ONE 10:e0133023 Measures the exposure across 83 crossover trials eligible for one network: 19 (23 percent) report a precision estimate that accounts for the pairing, 50 (60 percent) supply a point estimate and precision usable without further assumptions, 26 (31 percent) if a washout period is also required, and 16 (19 percent) report first-period results separately, so most crossover edges in such a network rest on an assumed within-participant correlation.
Elbourne, Altman, Higgins, Curtin, Worthington and Vail 2002, Meta-analyses involving cross-over trials: methodological issues, International Journal of Epidemiology 31:140-149 The standard reference for including crossover trials in a synthesis, setting out the options of imputing a within-participant correlation, using first-period data only, or excluding the trial, and treating the imputed correlation as a fixed input rather than as an estimated quantity carrying uncertainty.
Curtin, Elbourne and Altman 2002, Meta-analysis combining parallel and cross-over clinical trials. II: Binary outcomes, Statistics in Medicine 21:2145-2159 Derives the reconstruction of a crossover trial’s contrast and standard error from reported summaries under an assumed correlation, the calculation on which the assumption is operationalized; the resulting standard error is then conditioned on as known.
Curtin 2017, Meta-analysis combining parallel and crossover trials using generalised estimating equation method, Research Synthesis Methods 8 Combines aggregated results from crossover and parallel trials through a generalized estimating equation with a marginal estimation approach and can adjust for a carryover effect; it is a fixed-effects pairwise method, states that it is limited by the level of detail crossover reports usually give, and has not been carried into a network or evaluated for what it does to rankings.

Probable solution or research direction

The machinery exists outside PAIC, so the task is to port and test rather than to invent. Apply Bayesian model averaging as practiced in meta-analysis to population-adjusted networks, and state which predictive unit the stacking weights target, since that choice defines the estimand. Test whether projection predictive selection and robust Bayesian analysis over model and prior classes actually restore coverage here rather than assuming they do. For bridges, build likelihood-based weights where candidates imply different observed-data predictions, reserve elicited priors over assumption plausibility for the observationally equivalent case, and feed existing diagnostics such as bridge fragility into the reported interval instead of reporting them beside it.

Literature and prior-art check
open / supported-with-caveat. The entry carries no prior_work at all, which understates a mature adjacent literature. Model averaging as a response to selection uncertainty in evidence synthesis is established: Rover, Wandel & Friede (2019), Stat Med, describe ‘heavy-tailed mixture priors for effect estimation in meta-analysis, which effectively results in a model-averaging technique… robust in the sense that a potential prior-data conflict… is explicitly anticipated’; also Bartos et al., ‘Bayesian Model-Averaged Meta-Analysis in Medicine’ (arXiv 2110.01076). So ‘Reporting a single selected model understates uncertainty’ is supported but generic, not a PAIC finding. I could not retrieve any source stating the second claim, that model averaging in network settings requires choosing a valid predictive unit for stacking weights; it is defensible on general grounds about leave-one-out stacking in hierarchical data, but I found nobody writing it for NMA, so treat it as the authors’ own argument rather than a sourced one. I found no projection-predictive or robust-Bayes application to PAIC. The bridge half is the genuinely novel and correct part: candidate bridges are competing identifying assumptions rather than nested models, so likelihood-based weights do not exist and averaging would need a prior on assumption plausibility. I found no work doing that.
