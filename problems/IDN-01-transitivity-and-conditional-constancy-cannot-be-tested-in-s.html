Transitivity and conditional constancy cannot be tested in-sample

Identification, transitivity & conditional constancy
Very high priority
Partially addressed
Transitivity, consistency and conditional constancy restrict counterfactual quantities that are never jointly observed, so they cannot be verified nonparametrically from the data and passing any check does not establish transportability. Prespecifiable screens nevertheless exist: Spineli et al. (doi:10.1002/sim.70068) compute dissimilarities between treatment comparisons from study-level characteristics and cluster them to locate hot spots of potential intransitivity, backed by the tracenma database of 217 network datasets assembled to develop exactly this methodology, and Phillippo et al. (doi:10.1177/0272989X221117162) assess conditional constancy through residual heterogeneity and inconsistency, though only in sufficiently large networks. What survives is narrower than the source claimed: each screen tests one observable implication, a calibrated failure is evidence against at least one assumption without saying which, and no catalogue states which implications a population-adjusted comparison should prespecify or what action a failure triggers. The judgment also governs whether the analysis happens at all, and nothing governs the judgment: reviews that prespecify a network meta-analysis and then cannot assess whether potential effect modifiers are similar across comparisons abandon it on an unquantified reading, with no threshold, no requirement to report network estimates that were fitted and then withheld, and no convention for signaling the abandonment where readers will see it. Three 2017 Cochrane reviews from one group abandoned an identical prespecified Bayesian protocol this way, one of them after fitting the models and detecting inconsistency, and the Cochrane Central Editorial Unit required the phrase ‘attempted network meta-analysis’ to be removed from one title on the grounds that it might confuse readers. The screens are also not always executable, and nothing says when that matters: no rule states which covariates must be reported, by what fraction of the trials contributing to each comparison and at what level of detail, before a transitivity screen counts as runnable, nor what an analyst owes when it cannot be run, whether to proceed and label the network as assumption-laden, restrict to a subnetwork with adequate reporting, or decline. The consequence is visible divergence rather than acknowledged uncertainty: two Cochrane reviews abandoned planned network meta-analyses of hepatocellular carcinoma treatments because effect-modifier similarity could not be assessed, one of them across a connected 18-trial evidence base (doi:10.1002/14651858.CD011650.pub2, doi:10.1002/14651858.CD011649.pub2), while other teams synthesize and rank the same interventions, and neither the go nor the no-go decision can be checked against any stated criterion.

Why it is open

The restriction is on treatment contrasts in study and population cells that are never jointly observed, so no function of the observed data has power against every violation, and covariate balance or model fit can look excellent under severe residual confounding. The screens that exist do not close this. The dissimilarity and clustering approach works on reported study-level characteristics and returns a semi-objective judgment rather than a verdict, and in 209 real networks a likely intransitivity prevailed in all of them while statistical tests were feasible in only 61 percent and often disagreed with the dissimilarity reading. For population-adjusted comparisons the residual-heterogeneity route needs a network large enough to support it, which is not the setting where conditional constancy does the most identifying work.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
Spineli et al. 2024, Low awareness of the transitivity assumption in complex networks of interventions: a systematic survey from 721 network meta-analyses, BMC Medicine sources the previously unattributed claim that transitivity is examined poorly in practice: most reviews evaluated it statistically rather than conceptually (40 percent versus 12 percent before PRISMA-NMA, 54 percent versus 11 percent after), with consistency evaluation the most preferred device (34 percent to 47 percent)
Spineli 2024, An empirical study on 209 networks of treatments revealed intransitivity to be common and multiple statistical tests suboptimal to assess transitivity, BMC Medical Research Methodology empirical evidence that a likely intransitivity prevailed in all datasets examined and that statistical tests were feasible in only 61 percent of them and often conflicted with a study-dissimilarity reading
Spineli et al. 2025, Exploring the Transitivity Assumption in Network Meta-Analysis: A Novel Approach and Its Implications, Statistics in Medicine gives a prespecifiable quantitative transitivity screen from dissimilarity matrices and hierarchical clustering that detects hot spots of potential intransitivity and supports semi-objective judgments; it does not verify the assumption
tracenma R package v0.1.1 (2025), database for developing transitivity methodology in network meta-analysis; companion paper doi:10.1186/s12874-025-02634-x public benchmark database of 217 network meta-analysis datasets with extracted study-level candidate effect modifiers, built expressly to develop transitivity methodology
Phillippo et al. 2023, Validating the Assumptions of Population Adjustment: Application of Multilevel Network Meta-regression to a Network of Treatments for Plaque Psoriasis, Medical Decision Making assesses population-adjustment assumptions inside a nine-study network: splits the common class interaction into treatment-specific interactions one covariate at a time, checks conditional constancy through residual heterogeneity and inconsistency in sufficiently large networks, and reports that the shared-interaction restriction was required to identify the model where a treatment was informed only by aggregate data
Mantzoukis et al. 2017, Pharmacological interventions for acute hepatitis B infection: an attempted network meta-analysis, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews Shows what happens when the transitivity screen cannot be run: the planned network meta-analysis was abandoned because it was not possible to assess whether potential effect modifiers were similar across comparisons, and the Cochrane Central Editorial Unit then required the phrase ‘attempted network meta-analysis’ to be removed from the review title on the grounds that it might confuse readers, so the abandonment is invisible in the Cochrane Library record while PubMed retains it.
Lombardi et al. 2017, Pharmacological interventions for non-alcohol related fatty liver disease (NAFLD): an attempted network meta-analysis, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews A second review from the same group with the same prespecified Bayesian protocol, abandoned on the identical unquantified judgment that it was not possible to assess whether potential effect modifiers were similar across comparisons; 77 trials and 6287 participants were then synthesized pairwise instead.
Buzzetti et al. 2017, Pharmacological interventions for alcoholic liver disease (alcohol-related liver disease): an attempted network meta-analysis, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews The case where the models were fitted and the results withheld: in the subgroup where effect modifiers appeared reasonably similar across comparisons there was evidence of inconsistency, so the network results were not reported and 81 trials were synthesized pairwise. It shows the abandonment decision arising after fitting as well as before it.
van der Mark et al. 2012, A systematic review with attempted network meta-analysis of asthma therapy recommended for five to eighteen year olds in GINA guidelines, BMC Pulmonary Medicine An earlier attempted network meta-analysis, establishing that abandonment after prespecification predates the 2017 group of reviews and is not confined to one editorial group.
Boonpattharatthiti et al. 2025, Reporting and evaluation of assumptions and certainty of evidence in network meta-analyses, Research Synthesis Methods Surveys how assumptions and certainty of evidence are reported and evaluated across published network meta-analyses, which is the reporting-practice side of the same transitivity judgment.
Majumdar, Roccarina, Thorburn, Davidson, Tsochatzis and Gurusamy 2017, Management of people with early- or very early-stage hepatocellular carcinoma: an attempted network meta-analysis, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews CD011650 A planned network meta-analysis of 18 eligible randomized trials abandoned on the stated ground that it was not possible to assess whether the potential effect modifiers were similar across different comparisons, so performing a network meta-analysis in this scenario can be misleading; the review reverts to pairwise comparisons and issues no ranking.
Roccarina, Majumdar, Thorburn, Davidson, Tsochatzis and Gurusamy 2017, Management of people with intermediate-stage hepatocellular carcinoma: an attempted network meta-analysis, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews CD011649 The companion review reaching the same abstention on the same grounds in an adjacent evidence base, showing that the no-go decision is a repeated practice of one group rather than a single reviewer’s judgment, and still resting on no stated criterion.

Probable solution or research direction

The screening step no longer needs inventing, so the work is calibration and prespecification: characterize the operating characteristics of the dissimilarity and clustering screen against the tracenma datasets, and fix in advance which implications are checked, on what scale, and what a failure obliges the analyst to do. Around it, assemble the falsification devices that population adjustment still lacks, including held-out-trial contrast prediction, bridge deletion, negative controls, transported versus observed control-arm comparison, cross-trial interaction replication, and transport checks separated by calendar time, geography and setting. Report all of them with an explicit statement that passing does not establish transportability, and where falsification is impossible attach an explicit bias function to the restriction instead of asserting it.

Verdict. Partially addressed. Auditors agreed the problem is open and no counterevidence surfaced. Reopened by the full-text reading: 4 findings from 4 papers survived both the reader and an independent second reviewer, so the verdict moves from confirmed-open to partially-addressed.

The source said. “Standard ITC requires transitivity, consistency and a sensible effect scale, and PAIC adds conditional constancy of relative effects, yet these assumptions cannot be fully tested from the observed data. Research should therefore be redirected from impossible assumption tests towards prespecified falsification opportunities, accepting that success does not prove transportability while failure is highly informative.”

What was wrong with it. The source carried no citations and asserted both that there is no agreed catalogue of which implications to check or how to prespecify them and that a failed check is highly informative. The literature auditor sourced the practice claims (doi:10.1186/s12916-024-03322-1, doi:10.1186/s12874-024-02436-7) and found a prespecifiable transitivity screen already published (doi:10.1002/sim.70068, with cran:tracenma), so the entry is now sourced; the source-code auditor cut ‘highly informative’ back to evidence against at least one unidentified assumption, since a failure can equally reflect sampling variation, support failure, outcome-version differences or an invalid control.

open / overstated. The central nonidentification point is correct: observed data cannot verify equality of counterfactual treatment contrasts across unobserved study-treatment cells, and balance or model fit cannot rule out residual bias. However, C6 is a false scientific-versus-statistical dichotomy because these are formal statistical restrictions informed by science, sometimes with testable implications. C13 is materially too strong: a failed held-out prediction or negative control may reflect sampling variation, heterogeneity, support failure, outcome-version differences, model misspecification, or an invalid negative control. It is not automatically highly informative about transitivity. C14 additionally requires that withholding the trial preserve identification and that prediction and observation target the same population-specific estimand. C15 requires a valid negative control sharing the relevant bias pathway. C8-C10 are empirical literature claims outside this audit lens.

How the evidence falls in time

103 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2011 to 2026. Partial progress is on the record, and later work still calls this open. Both belong in view.

This problem is recurrent: three or more papers spanning at least eight years assert it independently. Sustained restatement by authors who mostly do not cite each other is the strongest evidence this reading can offer that a gap is real rather than one group’s framing.

It is also being closed in pieces: partial results come from more than one paper in more than one year, each covering a different part.
