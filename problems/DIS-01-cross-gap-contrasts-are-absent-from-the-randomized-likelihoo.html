Cross-gap contrasts are absent from the randomized likelihood

Disconnected networks & bridges
Very high priority
Confirmed open
In a connected network the relative effect between two treatments is identified through a path of randomized contrasts; when no such path exists the cross-subnetwork contrast lies outside the row space of the design and is absent from the randomized likelihood, so no amount of additional within-component data supplies it. Any finite estimate of that contrast comes from structure the analyst imposes: a proper prior, an equality restriction, a component or class model, external evidence, or a bridging trial. Goring et al. 2016 describe the setting and the standard fix of inserting a distribution for the unknown contrast, and Pedder et al. 2021 state that comparisons between disconnected treatments are not possible without strong assumptions. Unanchored MAIC and STC sit in this class; their requirement is conditional exchangeability of absolute potential outcomes across studies together with positivity, consistency, and correct outcome modeling, which Phillippo et al. 2018 describe as much stronger assumptions widely regarded as infeasible.

Statement

In a connected network the relative effect between two treatments is identified through a path of randomized contrasts; when no such path exists the cross-subnetwork contrast lies outside the row space of the design and is absent from the randomized likelihood, so no amount of additional within-component data supplies it. Any finite estimate of that contrast comes from structure the analyst imposes: a proper prior, an equality restriction, a component or class model, external evidence, or a bridging trial. Goring et al. 2016 describe the setting and the standard fix of inserting a distribution for the unknown contrast, and Pedder et al. 2021 state that comparisons between disconnected treatments are not possible without strong assumptions. Unanchored MAIC and STC sit in this class; their requirement is conditional exchangeability of absolute potential outcomes across studies together with positivity, consistency, and correct outcome modeling, which Phillippo et al. 2018 describe as much stronger assumptions widely regarded as infeasible.

Why it is open

The missing quantity is a structural property of the evidence graph rather than a sampling limitation: the cross-component contrast lies in a null direction of the design, so within-component precision can grow without ever touching it. Removing the anchor removes the structure that made cross-trial differences cancel, so the analysis must reconstruct an absolute-outcome comparison from studies never designed to be compared. Once any linking structure is added, Bayesian and frequentist software returns a finite estimate with an interval that reads to a decision maker exactly like an identified result, and posterior connectedness is not likelihood identification. Reporting guidance for population-adjusted comparisons now exists (Ishak et al. 2025), so this is not a total absence of standards, but that framework is scoped to population adjustment and does not require a disconnected-network result to name the structure that supplied the cross-gap contrast.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
Goring et al. 2016 canonical description of disconnected networks with neither direct evidence nor an indirect route between certain treatments, and of the recommended fix of inserting a distribution for the unknown contrast
Pedder et al. 2021 states that comparisons between disconnected treatments are not possible without making strong assumptions, and demonstrates a dose-response bridge that supplies them
Phillippo et al. 2018 sources the claim that unanchored comparisons make much stronger assumptions, which are widely regarded as infeasible
Ishak et al. 2025 publishes a reporting and risk-of-bias framework for population-adjusted indirect comparisons; it does not require a disconnected-network analysis to declare the structure that identifies the cross-gap contrast

Probable solution or research direction

Precede every disconnected-network result with an explicit statement that the randomized evidence identifies effects only within each connected component and that the cross-component comparison is identified only after imposing the stated bridge assumptions. Report the linking structure as part of the estimand definition rather than as a modeling footnote, and extend the existing population-adjustment reporting framework so it covers which parameter carries a disconnected gap. Drop the goal of a single algorithm combining five named linking devices: that combination is unbuilt only because nobody has proposed it, and the useful work is making whichever bridge is used auditable.

Verdict. Confirmed open. Auditors agreed the problem is open and no counterevidence surfaced.

The source said. “In a connected network the relative effect between two treatments is identified through a path of randomized contrasts; when no such path exists the cross-subnetwork contrast is not merely imprecise but absent from the randomized likelihood. Literature and prior-art check
open / supported-with-caveat. prior_work is empty, so I retrieved the canonical sources myself. Goring et al. 2016 (Res Synth Methods) frames disconnected networks exactly as the entry does and states the standard fix is to insert a distribution for the unknown contrast. Pedder et al. 2021 states outright that comparisons between disconnected treatments are not possible without strong assumptions. Phillippo et al. 2018 supplies the unanchored-MAIC/STC claim verbatim. The structural non-identification claim is correct and cannot be solved by any paper, so open is right. Caveats: two claims are absence claims I cannot verify by finding nothing, namely that disconnected aggregate-data methods are ‘still niche in practice’ and that ‘no network-connecting algorithm currently combines’ five named devices. The second is trivially true only because nobody has proposed that particular five-way combination; it should not be read as a research gap of consequence. The entry should cite Goring 2016, which is the reference a disconnected-networks reader will expect first. How the evidence falls in time

26 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2013 to 2026. Every paper found here asserts the problem is open; none reports progress.

This problem is recurrent: three or more papers spanning at least eight years assert it independently. Sustained restatement by authors who mostly do not cite each other is the strongest evidence this reading can offer that a gap is real rather than one group’s framing.
