Intervals omit selection, reconstruction and target-summary error

Decision analysis, regulation & reporting
Very high priority
Partially addressed
The estimated-weights component of this problem is solved and evaluated: Chandler and Proskorovsky 2024 (10.1002/jrsm.1759) compared four families of MAIC variance estimator across 108 scenarios and give guidance by overlap, effective sample size and outcome type, following Remiro-Azocar et al. 2021 (10.1002/jrsm.1511), which had shown the robust sandwich estimator understates variability at small ESS. What remains unpropagated is model-selection uncertainty where selection used the analyzed data, pseudo-IPD reconstruction uncertainty, target-summary uncertainty, and any decomposition separating sampling from prior and structural contributions. Bridge uncertainty belongs on that list only with a caveat: it is structural rather than sampling-based and should be reported through separate sensitivity or tipping analysis unless a defensible probability model supports joint propagation. The claim that standard software returns one interval with no components is also not true of cpaic, an unreleased GitHub development package not on CRAN, which re-estimates MAIC weights inside an arm-stratified bootstrap, preserves multi-arm covariance, and carries an integration-error diagnostic for component ML-NMR.

DEC-11 Partially addressed Very high priority Promising

Statement

The estimated-weights component of this problem is solved and evaluated: Chandler and Proskorovsky 2024 (10.1002/jrsm.1759) compared four families of MAIC variance estimator across 108 scenarios and give guidance by overlap, effective sample size and outcome type, following Remiro-Azocar et al. 2021 (10.1002/jrsm.1511), which had shown the robust sandwich estimator understates variability at small ESS. What remains unpropagated is model-selection uncertainty where selection used the analyzed data, pseudo-IPD reconstruction uncertainty, target-summary uncertainty, and any decomposition separating sampling from prior and structural contributions. Bridge uncertainty belongs on that list only with a caveat: it is structural rather than sampling-based and should be reported through separate sensitivity or tipping analysis unless a defensible probability model supports joint propagation. The claim that standard software returns one interval with no components is also not true of cpaic, an unreleased GitHub development package not on CRAN, which re-estimates MAIC weights inside an arm-stratified bootstrap, preserves multi-arm covariance, and carries an integration-error diagnostic for component ML-NMR.

Why it is open

Interval estimates are conditional on the selected model, so a selection step carried out on the analyzed data leaves the reported interval too narrow, and no ITC reporting convention requires selection uncertainty to be accounted for. Each remaining component enters at a different stage of a multi-step pipeline, and the final stage that produces the interval has no access to what earlier stages introduced. Numerical integration error is a deterministic approximation error, so the fix is bounding and convergence assessment rather than treatment as another random variance component; the existing diagnostic traces nested approximations against the final grid rather than against the exact integral and is unavailable for survival models.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
Chandler and Proskorovsky 2024 108-scenario simulation comparing four families of MAIC variance estimator; finds raw-weight conventional estimators understate variability under poor or moderate overlap, ESS-weighted conventional estimators accurate across most scenarios, and sandwich estimators downward-biased at small ESS without finite-sample adjustment.
Remiro-Azocar, Heath and Baio 2021 Earlier evidence that the robust sandwich variance estimator underestimates variability in MAIC where effective sample sizes are small.
cpaic (development package, audited at commit 9d150e9) Unreleased development package on GitHub, confirmed by an auditor not to be on CRAN. Re-estimates MAIC weights and refits the outcome model inside an arm-stratified bootstrap, preserves multi-arm covariance through netmeta for the frequentist bridge and the standard 0.5 baseline-shift correlation for random effects, and provides a nested-Sobol integration-error diagnostic; selection, target-summary, pseudo-IPD and bridge-uncertainty decomposition remain unimplemented.

Probable solution or research direction

Adopt the published variance-estimation guidance for weights instead of treating it as open, and extend propagation to the stages still conditioned on: pseudo-IPD reconstruction, target summaries including their covariance, and model selection where the selection used the analyzed data. Require full model specification with priors and random-effects structure, explicit separation of within-study from between-study effect modification, justification of shared versus treatment- or component-specific interactions, and presentation of substantively plausible alternative models. Report structural bridge uncertainty and numerical error separately, as sensitivity regions and convergence bounds, rather than merging them into a single interval.

Verification

Verdict. Partially addressed. Part of this problem is addressed by existing work; the entry names which part.

The source titled this. “Reported intervals omit selection, weighting and bridge uncertainty” The title lists weighting uncertainty as unpropagated, but the audit found the estimated-weights variance question settled across a 108-scenario evaluation with actionable guidance.

The source said. “Reported ITC and PAIC intervals are computed conditional on one selected model and on upstream inputs treated as fixed. Model specification and the selection process are not fully reported, and uncertainty from estimated weights, multi-arm covariance, pseudo-IPD reconstruction, target summaries, priors, numerical integration and the bridge itself is neither propagated nor decomposed.”

What was wrong with it. The source treated estimated-weight uncertainty in MAIC as unaddressed and said standard software offers no decomposition. The literature auditor found the variance-estimation question settled by a 108-scenario evaluation with actionable guidance (10.1002/jrsm.1759), and GPT-5.6 Sol found weight bootstrapping, multi-arm covariance and an integration-error diagnostic already implemented in cpaic and objected that structural bridge uncertainty should not be folded into a confidence interval.

Dissent. codex dissented (overstated): C1 applies when model selection used the analyzed data, not to a genuinely prespecified model; its claimed frequency is not established here. C2-C3 are correct because between-study interaction gradients are ecologically confounded and shared interactions are structural assumptions. C4 is warranted for substantively plausible alternatives, not every imaginable model. C5 should say random-effects structures and priors can materially affect conclusions and must be reported. C6-C8 are sound when those stages are estimated, with target-summary covariance also needed where applicable. C7’s necessity is exact, but its frequency claim is unverified. C9 is important, but bridge uncertainty is structural and cannot be inserted into a confidence interval without a defensible probability model; sensitivity regions or tipping analyses should ordinarily remain separate. C10 should require convergence assessment and control of numerical error, not automatic treatment as a stochastic variance component. The blanket software claim is false for cpaic: cMAIC re-estimates weights inside an arm-stratified bootstrap, network engines preserve multi-arm dependence, and cML-NMR has an integration-error diagnostic. Selection, target-summary, pseudo-IPD, and full bridge-uncertainty decomposition remain unresolved.

Literature and prior-art check
partially-solved / supported-with-caveat. One component of this bundle has been formally settled. Chandler & Proskorovsky 2024 (Res Synth Methods, doi 10.1002/jrsm.1759, PMID 39323097) ran a 108-scenario Monte Carlo study comparing four families of variance estimator for MAIC (conventional with raw weights, conventional with ESS-rescaled weights, robust sandwich variants, bootstrap) across anchored and unanchored designs and binary and time-to-event outcomes, and gives concrete guidance: raw-weight conventional estimators understate variability under poor or moderate overlap, sandwich estimators are downward-biased at small ESS unless finite-sample-adjusted, bootstrapping is unstable at small ESS. Remiro-Azocar et al. 2021 (doi 10.1002/jrsm.1511) had already shown ‘the robust sandwich variance estimator underestimates variability where effective sample sizes are small’. So ‘uncertainty from estimated weights should be accounted for in MAIC standard errors’ is no longer an open problem; it is a solved-and-evaluated question with published recommendations. The remaining components, model-selection uncertainty, pseudo-IPD reconstruction uncertainty, target-summary uncertainty, multi-arm covariance preservation, numerical integration error, and a decomposition separating sampling from bridge and prior uncertainty, I found no work addressing, and they remain open. How the evidence falls in time

10 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2013 to 2026. The most recent paper to touch this reports progress on it, and every paper calling it open is older.

It is also being closed in pieces: partial results come from more than one paper in more than one year, each covering a different part.

Does the later work answer the earlier work? Put to an independent reviewer with the older and newer claims side by side, it judged that the later work answers a component of it, leaving a named part (high confidence). The later work substantially addresses uncertainty from estimated MAIC weights, including comparative evaluation and practical limits under poor overlap. It does not propagate the other named sources of uncertainty into the reported interval. What remains: Propagation of data-driven model-selection uncertainty, pseudo-IPD reconstruction uncertainty, target-summary uncertainty, and a defensible decomposition of sampling, structural, and prior contributions; structural bridge uncertainty still needs sensitivity or tipping analyses unless probabilistically modeled.
