No agreed parameterization or format for unanchored PAIC sensitivity

Quantitative bias analysis for unanchored PAIC
Very high priority
Confirmed open
Unanchored PAICs depend on cross-study exchangeability and transportability assumptions that are strong and partly untestable: Phillippo et al. 2017 (doi:10.1177/0272989X17725740), the peer-reviewed account of NICE DSU TSD 18, state that unanchored comparisons make much stronger assumptions, which are widely regarded as infeasible. The report’s rule, that no unanchored PAIC receives a strong causal interpretation without a formal QBA or partial-identification analysis or an explicit exploratory label, is a reporting safeguard rather than a condition for identification: QBA is neither mathematically necessary when the identifying assumptions hold nor sufficient to justify a causal claim, and it never establishes the absence of bias outside the specified model or range. Residual bias is unestimable from the usual observed data law without auxiliary information, so negative controls, proxies, validation data or additional structural restrictions are what would change that, and there is still no agreed parameterization, threshold or reporting format for the sensitivity analysis the rule would require.

Statement

Unanchored PAICs depend on cross-study exchangeability and transportability assumptions that are strong and partly untestable: Phillippo et al. 2017 (doi:10.1177/0272989X17725740), the peer-reviewed account of NICE DSU TSD 18, state that unanchored comparisons make much stronger assumptions, which are widely regarded as infeasible. The report’s rule, that no unanchored PAIC receives a strong causal interpretation without a formal QBA or partial-identification analysis or an explicit exploratory label, is a reporting safeguard rather than a condition for identification: QBA is neither mathematically necessary when the identifying assumptions hold nor sufficient to justify a causal claim, and it never establishes the absence of bias outside the specified model or range. Residual bias is unestimable from the usual observed data law without auxiliary information, so negative controls, proxies, validation data or additional structural restrictions are what would change that, and there is still no agreed parameterization, threshold or reporting format for the sensitivity analysis the rule would require.

Why it is open

Residual bias in an unanchored comparison is a function of unmeasured prognostic structure, so within the usual data law it can only be bounded by assumption, and there is no agreed parameterization, prior or reporting format for that bias in the ITC setting, so even a willing analyst has no standard instrument. Enforcement depends on journals and appraisal bodies while unanchored PAICs continue to be submitted as primary comparative evidence with no quantification of residual bias, often where no alternative exists, so the pressure is toward a point estimate rather than a bias range. There is no agreed threshold for what counts as a formal QBA, so a token one-parameter analysis could satisfy the letter of the rule. Labeling a result exploratory also removes the basis for a reimbursement decision that still has to be made, which creates pressure against applying the rule exactly where it matters most.

Probable solution or research direction

Adopt the rule as a reporting safeguard rather than as an identification criterion: require unanchored submissions to state the exchangeability and transportability assumptions explicitly and, for decision-grade use, to accompany the estimate with QBA or partial identification over substantively justified departures, qualifying the causal interpretation as exploratory where adequate sensitivity analysis is unavailable. Define a minimum standard for what qualifies, so a token one-parameter analysis does not satisfy it, and state in the output that the analysis characterizes robustness under its own assumptions rather than establishing the absence of bias. Pair the exploratory label with decision procedures that can act on bounds, and where auxiliary information exists, whether negative controls, proxies, validation data or defensible structural restrictions, use it rather than treating residual bias as unaddressable in principle.

open / overstated. The underlying concern remains open, but the central rule is too categorical as a technical claim. An unanchored cross-trial contrast generally requires conditional exchangeability or transportability of potential-outcome levels across studies, plus consistency, positivity, estimand alignment, and valid estimation. This makes C2 and the technical content of C3 directionally correct when comparing otherwise sound anchored and unanchored ITCs, although no design is universally the least credible. C4 and C8 are correct: sensitivity methods characterize robustness only under their specified assumptions and cannot exclude bias outside the modeled mechanisms or parameter ranges. C7 confuses a prudent reporting safeguard with a condition for causal identification. QBA or partial identification is neither mathematically necessary when the identifying assumptions hold nor sufficient to justify a strong causal interpretation. The claim that residual bias can never be estimated from the data is valid only for the usual observed IPD-plus-aggregate-data law without auxiliary information, negative controls, proxies, validation data, or additional structural restrictions. C5 and C6 are empirical practice claims that cannot be established from statistical theory alone. At cpaic@9d150e9, NAMESPACE exposes no general residual-bias QBA for unanchored MAIC or STC: prior_sensitivity varies cML-NMR priors, while bridge_fragility addresses bounded component main-effect drift. This supports the package-level gap but cannot establish field-wide absence or routine practice. C1 is a provenance flag rather than a methodological claim. The issue is genuinely high severity when an unanchored estimate drives a decision, but not automatically for explicitly exploratory work.
