Deciding which covariates are true effect modifiers

Covariates: selection, target moments, measurement
Very high priority
Partially addressed
There is no agreed default for choosing the effect-modifier set: the right set depends on the treatment contrast, estimand, effect scale, design and available support, and in the small samples typical of these analyses significance screening is unstable while balancing on every available covariate can destroy overlap and exhaust the information available for interaction terms. Reviews document what applied work does: NICE technology appraisals selected covariates by availability, expert opinion, effective sample size, statistical significance or cross-validation (Phillippo et al. 2019, doi:10.1017/S0266462319000333); of 117 oncology MAICs only 3 met all NICE recommendations, with evidence of effect-modifier status among the least reported items (Farinasso 2025, doi:10.1016/j.vhri.2025.101088); and no study among 27 CAR-T indirect comparisons described its identification methodology (Neumann 2026, doi:10.57264/cer-2026-0033). Adjustment sets are not uniformly small: those reviews report anywhere from no adjustment to 19 covariates, and a median of 6 (range 1 to 13) for unanchored analyses. A formal causal covariate-selection procedure does exist for transporting experimental results (Egami and Hartman, arxiv:1909.02669), but it assumes richer target data than an aggregate-data comparator supplies, and it has not been benchmarked for this setting.

Statement

There is no agreed default for choosing the effect-modifier set: the right set depends on the treatment contrast, estimand, effect scale, design and available support, and in the small samples typical of these analyses significance screening is unstable while balancing on every available covariate can destroy overlap and exhaust the information available for interaction terms. Reviews document what applied work does: NICE technology appraisals selected covariates by availability, expert opinion, effective sample size, statistical significance or cross-validation (Phillippo et al. 2019, doi:10.1017/S0266462319000333); of 117 oncology MAICs only 3 met all NICE recommendations, with evidence of effect-modifier status among the least reported items (Farinasso 2025, doi:10.1016/j.vhri.2025.101088); and no study among 27 CAR-T indirect comparisons described its identification methodology (Neumann 2026, doi:10.57264/cer-2026-0033). Adjustment sets are not uniformly small: those reviews report anywhere from no adjustment to 19 covariates, and a median of 6 (range 1 to 13) for unanchored analyses. A formal causal covariate-selection procedure does exist for transporting experimental results (Egami and Hartman, arxiv:1909.02669), but it assumes richer target data than an aggregate-data comparator supplies, and it has not been benchmarked for this setting.

Why it is open

Interaction terms carry far less information than main effects, so a data-driven modifier set fluctuates across resamples and the downstream interval is conditional on a selection event that is never reported. Balancing on everything trades that instability for a support problem: extra balancing constraints usually make the weights harder to satisfy and cut effective sample size, though not monotonically, and extra interaction terms consume information the network may not hold. Clinical elicitation, the remaining route, is incomplete and not free of the analyst’s interest in the result. Because the rationale is seldom documented the same evidence base yields different adjustment sets in different submissions, and getting the set wrong has measured consequences: all population adjustment methods incur bias when an effect modifier is missing (Phillippo et al. 2020, doi:10.1002/sim.8759).

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
Phillippo et al., 2019 Reviews population adjustment in NICE technology appraisals and records that covariates were included ‘based on: availability, expert opinion, effective sample size, statistical significance, or cross-validation’; the audit uses it to source the ad hoc and significance-screening claims, and it reports a median of 6 covariates (range 1 to 13) for unanchored analyses.
Farinasso, 2025 Scoping review of 117 oncology MAICs focused on reporting quality; only 3 fulfilled all NICE recommendations, and evidence of effect-modifier status and the distribution of weights were among the least reported items.
Neumann, 2026 Systematic review of 27 CAR-T indirect comparisons; covariates were chosen from clinical expertise or literature review but ‘no study provided a detailed description of the identification methodology’, and adjustment ranged from none to 19 covariates.
Egami and Hartman, 2021 Formal causal covariate-selection procedure based on estimated separating sets for generalizing experimental results; adjacent prior art that assumes richer target data than an aggregate-data comparator provides.
Phillippo et al., 2020 Simulation study of anchored population adjustment methods; all methods incur bias when an effect modifier is missing, which is the consequence of getting the modifier set wrong.

Probable solution or research direction

Prespecify the selection process and record it: causal and clinical knowledge, cross-trial interaction evidence, prognostic strength where the design is unanchored or the strategy is precision-oriented, measurement comparability, expected imbalance between source and target, support and estimability consequences, and predeclared sensitivity sets over alternative modifier lists. The gap to fill is not a way to encode nonlinear covariate effects, which splines and basis expansions already do, but a way to choose and estimate them when interaction information is sparse. Adapt and benchmark the existing separating-set procedure (arxiv:1909.02669) for the case where the target is known only through published summaries, and report uncertainty for the whole procedure including selection, whether by sample splitting, cross-fitting, resampling the entire selection procedure, selective inference, shrinkage or model averaging. Minimum covariate reporting sets for aggregate-data publications would make any of this auditable.

Verification

Verdict. Partially addressed. Auditors agreed the problem is open and no counterevidence surfaced. Reopened by the full-text reading: 3 findings from 3 papers survived both the reader and an independent second reviewer, so the verdict moves from confirmed-open to partially-addressed.

The source said. “Effect-modifier transport is the entire reason population adjustment exists, yet there is no defensible default for choosing the modifier set: significance-based screening rests on underpowered interaction tests, adjusting for every available covariate destroys overlap and can leave the interaction model unidentified, and clinical elicitation is incomplete. Applied work uses small ad hoc covariate sets with weak overlap diagnostics and poorly reported selection rationale.”

What was wrong with it. The claim was unattributed in the source, which also said applied work uses ‘small ad hoc covariate sets’; the literature auditor supplied real references, so the ad hoc-selection, weak-diagnostic and poor-rationale claims are now sourced to three reviews (doi:10.1017/S0266462319000333, doi:10.1016/j.vhri.2025.101088, doi:10.57264/cer-2026-0033), the last of which contradicts ‘small’ by reporting adjustment sets that range from none to 19 covariates. The code auditor struck two further claims: splines and basis expansions already encode nonlinear covariate effects, so only their selection and estimation under sparse data is unresolved, and honest sample splitting is one valid inferential strategy among several rather than a necessity.

How the evidence falls in time

29 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2012 to 2025. Partial progress is on the record, and later work still calls this open. Both belong in view.

This problem is recurrent: three or more papers spanning at least eight years assert it independently. Sustained restatement by authors who mostly do not cite each other is the strongest evidence this reading can offer that a gap is real rather than one group’s framing.

It is also being closed in pieces: partial results come from more than one paper in more than one year, each covering a different part.
