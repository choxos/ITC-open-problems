No versioned intervention ontology maps onto CNMA component matrices

Component methods (CNMA and component PAIC)
Very high priority
Overstated
No component ontology is wired into component NMA or component PAIC software. Versioned, machine-readable intervention ontologies built for evidence synthesis do exist and record dose, delivery, and setting: the Behaviour Change Intervention Ontology upper level specifies 42 entities, the Mode of Delivery Ontology gives 65 modes across 15 upper classes with measured inter-rater reliability, and the Intervention Setting Ontology adds 72 entities. Quantitative dose is also no longer always a silent string match, since dose-response NMA models dose continuously and ships on CRAN as netdose. What is missing is a reusable, version-preserving and software-supported mapping from structured intervention ontologies onto CNMA component matrices, and any hierarchical equivalence-class model that lets related component versions borrow strength without being forced to share a parameter. The same vocabulary gap has its sharpest consequence on the comparator side, where placebo, sham, waiting list, no treatment, treatment as usual and standard care are routinely merged into one node and the merge is often what makes the network connect at all. Control-arm content is systematically unreported, so the mapping from arm to node is made from an outcome-blind guess at study content; in a 123-trial psychotherapy network the odds ratio for response between the strongest and weakest control condition is 5.42 (3.35 to 8.77), larger than several of the active contrasts the network exists to estimate, and design-by-treatment and side-splitting tests do not detect it because it is a property of the node’s definition rather than a disagreement between evidence paths.

CMP-05 Overstated Very high priority Emerging

Statement

No component ontology is wired into component NMA or component PAIC software. Versioned, machine-readable intervention ontologies built for evidence synthesis do exist and record dose, delivery, and setting: the Behaviour Change Intervention Ontology upper level specifies 42 entities, the Mode of Delivery Ontology gives 65 modes across 15 upper classes with measured inter-rater reliability, and the Intervention Setting Ontology adds 72 entities. Quantitative dose is also no longer always a silent string match, since dose-response NMA models dose continuously and ships on CRAN as netdose. What is missing is a reusable, version-preserving and software-supported mapping from structured intervention ontologies onto CNMA component matrices, and any hierarchical equivalence-class model that lets related component versions borrow strength without being forced to share a parameter. The same vocabulary gap has its sharpest consequence on the comparator side, where placebo, sham, waiting list, no treatment, treatment as usual and standard care are routinely merged into one node and the merge is often what makes the network connect at all. Control-arm content is systematically unreported, so the mapping from arm to node is made from an outcome-blind guess at study content; in a 123-trial psychotherapy network the odds ratio for response between the strongest and weakest control condition is 5.42 (3.35 to 8.77), larger than several of the active contrasts the network exists to estimate, and design-by-treatment and side-splitting tests do not detect it because it is a property of the node’s definition rather than a disagreement between evidence paths.

Why it is open

The bridge rests on the claim that a component means the same thing in both subnetworks, and that claim is currently established by matching treatment-label strings rather than by linking to any of the existing structured standards. Published trial reports rarely record dose intensity, schedule, or delivery fidelity in a machine-comparable way, so even a careful analyst usually cannot verify component equivalence from the available evidence, and once two implementations share a label the model treats their effects as one parameter. Storing the component matrix makes a coding decision reproducible but does not make it justified, and no model exists that lets a version of a component share information with its siblings without being forced equal to them.

Work What it contributes

Caldwell et al. 2025, Prevention Science Separates six control types in a school-based anxiety and depression prevention network and reports that the apparent benefit in targeted populations disappears, with waiting list acting as a nocebo relative to no intervention and attention control, concluding that conflating controls in meta-analysis is not appropriate.
Pratt et al. 2020, Systematic Reviews Scoping review of 89 complementary and alternative medicine network meta-analyses that names the merging of sham, placebo and waiting-list controls as requiring consideration and as previously shown to introduce bias into network results, and reports that only 34.8 percent adequately reported their methods for exploring network geometry.
Michopoulos et al. 2021, Journal of Clinical Epidemiology In a 123-trial psychotherapy network for depression, the odds ratio for response between the strongest and weakest control condition is 5.42 (3.35 to 8.77), larger than several of the active contrasts the network exists to estimate, and care as usual had to be excluded because its content could not be pinned down. Concludes that various control conditions should no longer be lumped into one comparison group.
Holper and Hengartner 2020, BMC Psychiatry Treats placebo arms of short-term antidepressant trials as drug-specific nodes rather than one node, which shows the cost of splitting: comparisons disconnect and need an exchangeability bridge, and placebo-versus-placebo contrasts become purely indirect so consistency cannot be checked.
Molloy et al. 2018, Health Psychology Review Primer on network meta-analysis for complex interventions that names control-group content as a considerable threat to transitivity which is difficult to identify because of poor reporting, and places lumping and splitting at the data-extraction step of its own recommended workflow, where planned approaches may be modified in light of the collected data.

Probable solution or research direction

Map the existing intervention ontologies onto CNMA component matrices rather than building a new ontology, so that dose, schedule, delivery, and setting attributes become the source of the component coding and the equivalence decisions become auditable data that can be rerun under alternative groupings. Use dose-response models where dose is the dimension that varies, since that machinery is released and validated. The genuinely new work is a hierarchical equivalence-class or embedding model in which related component versions borrow strength with a shrinkage parameter rather than being forced to share one.

Verification

Verdict. Overstated. 2 independent auditors judged the claim as stated too strong. No component ontology is wired into component NMA or component PAIC software: existing versioned, machine-readable intervention ontologies (BCIO and its mode-of-delivery and setting modules) record dose, schedule and delivery, but nothing maps them onto CNMA component matrices, and no hierarchical equivalence-class model lets related component versions borrow strength without being forced to share a parameter.

The source titled this. “Component ontology is label-based, not versioned or quantitative” The title says component ontology is not versioned or quantitative, but the audit found three published versioned OWL ontologies with dose and delivery metadata plus continuous dose modeling in netdose.

The source said. “Components in current component NMA and component PAIC are defined by text labels. The same label can hide differences in dose, schedule, formulation, device, adherence, provider skill, and cointerventions, so two studies nominally sharing a component may not share a parameter.”

What was wrong with it. The source said no versioned component ontology with dose and intensity metadata exists and that equivalence decisions are analyst judgment buried in code. The literature auditor found three published, versioned OWL ontologies covering exactly that metadata (BCIO, Mode of Delivery, Intervention Setting); Grok 4.5 added that dose can be modeled continuously via dose-response NMA and the CRAN package netdose; and GPT-5.6 Sol found that choxos/cpaic@9d150e9 stores the chosen C.matrix in the fitted network object, so the coding is recorded and rerunnable even though no ontology backs it.

Dissent. grok dissented (supported-with-caveat): Label-based component coding is still the default in CNMA practice (Veroniki et al. 2026 treat component definition/coding as a critical node-making decision and point to TIDieR for richer trial reporting, not a machine-versioned ontology). Partial prior art does exist on one dimension of versioning: quantitative dose. Pedder et al. 2021 show dose–response MBNMA can link disconnected networks via shared dose-response assumptions; Petropoulou, Rücker, and Schwarzer 2026 introduce frequentist DR-NMA with linear/nonlinear dose functions and ship the CRAN package netdose (2026). Watt et al. 2022 discuss hierarchical dose nodes. Wigle et al. arXiv:2605.15142 (2026) address estimability and ranking hierarchies of components/treatments, not versioned implementation attributes. No versioned component ontology covering dose, schedule, formulation, device, adherence, provider skill, and cointerventions with auditable equivalence classes was found for CNMA/PAIC. Hierarchical embeddings that share strength without forcing equality of versions were not found in CNMA software. Caveat: dose-response methods partially solve quantitative intensity; they do not close the broader ontology claim.

Literature and prior-art check
partially-solved / overstated. The claim ‘No versioned component ontology with dose and intensity metadata currently exists’ is false as a field-level statement. The Behaviour Change Intervention Ontology, built inside the Wellcome-funded Human Behaviour-Change Project explicitly for evidence synthesis, is a versioned, machine-readable OWL ontology whose upper level specifies 42 entities covering intervention content and delivery; intervention content is decomposed into behaviour change techniques plus dose, and the companion Mode of Delivery Ontology gives 65 modes across 15 upper classes with measured inter-rater reliability (kappa 0.80 for trained annotators), with a further Setting Ontology of 72 entities. The TIDieR reporting checklist independently mandates dose, schedule and delivery reporting (I did not retrieve it, so I give no locator). What is genuinely absent is any linkage of these ontologies to CNMA/CPAIC component coding, and any equivalence-class model that lets related component versions share a prior without being forced equal. The entry has zero prior_work, which is the more serious defect: this is an area with a decade of published ontology engineering. Cites doi:10.12688/wellcomeopenres.15902.2: A versioned, machine-readable ontology of intervention components built for evidence synthesis already exists; it is not yet connected to CNMA.. Cites doi:10.12688/wellcomeopenres.15906.2: Supplies the ‘delivery/formulation/device’ metadata layer the entry says does not exist, in OWL, with tested inter-rater reliability.. Cites doi:10.12688/wellcomeopenres.15904.1: Context/setting metadata layer, same programme; further evidence the ontology infrastructure exists..
GPT-5.6 Sol, technical and source-code lens
partially-solved / overstated. C1 is source-confirmed for cpaic, not a mathematical necessity of component NMA: build_C_matrix() splits treatment strings on sep.comps and creates a binary C matrix. C2 is technically correct. cpaic has no structured fields for dose, schedule, formulation, device, adherence, delivery, or hierarchical equivalence, supporting the package-specific versions of C3 and C4; software-wide claims are outside this audit. C5 applies when component coefficients receive a causal interpretation; purely statistical prediction requires the coding to support the assumed common additive surface, not necessarily a separately manipulable causal component. The report overstates that equivalence decisions cannot be recorded or audited: cpaic stores the treatment labels, component names, separator, and exact C.matrix in the network object, and these inputs can be version-controlled or rerun under alternative labels. Distinct versions can already be encoded as distinct component labels, albeit without structured metadata or borrowing. C6 is a reasonable data-governance direction. For C7, embeddings could support graded borrowing, but an embedding alone neither defines causal equivalence nor guarantees valid effect sharing. How the evidence falls in time

42 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2018 to 2026. Progress and a fresh assertion of openness land in the same year.

This problem is recurrent: three or more papers spanning at least eight years assert it independently. Sustained restatement by authors who mostly do not cite each other is the strongest evidence this reading can offer that a gap is real rather than one group’s framing.
