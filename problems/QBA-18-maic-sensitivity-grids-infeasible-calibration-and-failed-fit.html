MAIC sensitivity grids: infeasible calibration and failed fits

Quantitative bias analysis for unanchored PAIC
Very high priority
Partially addressed
With normalized nonnegative weights a matched target moment vector must lie in the convex hull of the source moment vectors, so an assumed moment for an omitted covariate outside that hull cannot be matched exactly, and finite strictly positive exponential tilting generally requires the relative interior, with boundary points needing zero weights or a limiting solution. Ren et al. 2025 (doi:10.1017/rsm.2025.13) give the applied version, restricting their published QBA to STC because a MAIC extension would suffer extreme effective-sample-size loss and might fail to produce feasible weights where overlap on the omitted covariate is limited. QBA-18 Partially addressed Very high priority Emerging

Statement

With normalized nonnegative weights a matched target moment vector must lie in the convex hull of the source moment vectors, so an assumed moment for an omitted covariate outside that hull cannot be matched exactly, and finite strictly positive exponential tilting generally requires the relative interior, with boundary points needing zero weights or a limiting solution. Ren et al. 2025 (doi:10.1017/rsm.2025.13) give the applied version, restricting their published QBA to STC because a MAIC extension would suffer extreme effective-sample-size loss and might fail to produce feasible weights where overlap on the omitted covariate is limited. What software does with such a point is the open part: infeasibility, failed fits, divergent transitions and extreme weights are worked around or discarded rather than reported, and no implementation returns a feasibility region alongside the effect surface.

Why it is open

An assumed target moment outside the source support means the desired counterfactual cannot be reached by reweighting the observed data, which is scientifically informative, but default software behavior treats it as a numerical nuisance to be worked around, so the surviving output is a selected sample of the sensitivity region rather than the whole of it. Current implementations report one effective sample size and a point estimate, so an unsupported scenario cannot be distinguished from an inconvenient failed run and severe effective-sample-size loss goes unreported. Trimming to rescue a fit silently redefines the target as an overlap population and nothing in the output flags the switch, while ad hoc weight truncation biases estimation of the original target without defining a clean replacement estimand. There is no accepted abstention or bounding rule and no output object that presents a failure map next to the effect surface.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
Ren et al. 2025, Research Synthesis Methods States that a sensitivity analysis over the full spectrum of possible values for the omitted covariate includes cases with very limited overlap, where MAIC would suffer an extreme reduction of the effective sample size and may even fail to produce feasible weights. This is why the published QBA covers only STC.

Probable solution or research direction

Develop sensitivity-adjusted calibration for MAIC that returns a feasibility map alongside the effect surface, reporting effective sample size and local effective sample size, weight concentration, residual target-moment imbalance and a support measure such as distance to the support boundary or a depth statistic with its norm and scaling stated, and that distinguishes exact from approximate balance in the output. Specify abstention behavior so software flags or declines an unsupported assumed moment rather than coercing a finite answer, and supply a bound only where the additional outcome and selection assumptions needed for one are stated. Make the feasibility and convergence map a required component of the QBA object and the published report, including the fraction of the sensitivity region in which the analysis could not be computed.

Verification

Verdict. Partially addressed. Auditors agreed the problem is open and no counterevidence surfaced. Reopened by the full-text reading: 3 findings from 3 papers survived both the reader and an independent second reviewer, so the verdict moves from confirmed-open to partially-addressed.

The source said. “When an omitted covariate’s target moment is unknown, MAIC QBA varies an assumed moment vector and re-solves the calibration; assumed moments outside the convex hull of source support make exact balance infeasible, yet software either coerces a finite answer or errors out. Failed fits, infeasible calibration, divergent transitions and extreme weights are silently discarded rather than reported as results.”

What was wrong with it. GPT-5.6 Sol qualified the convex-hull argument and narrowed two claims: an infeasible calibration means exact reweighting of the observed empirical support is impossible rather than that the target counterfactual is logically unsupported, and finite-sample support must be distinguished from optimizer failure. It also found false the claim that approximate balance changes the estimand: trimming to an explicit overlap population changes the estimand, while approximate balance generally changes the estimator and leaves residual imbalance. Returning a bound instead of a coerced answer requires additional outcome and selection assumptions rather than following automatically.

Literature and prior-art check
open / supported. The single citation is accurate. Ren et al. 2025 state, as the reason they present QBA only for STC, that ‘the sensitivity analysis should assess the impact of the full spectrum of possible values for U and this would include the cases where there would be very limited overlap on U between the two studies. MAIC approach would suffer an extreme reduction of the effect sample size and may even fail to produce feasible weights’, and again in the discussion that ‘an extension of the MAIC approach would lead to a large reduction of the effective sample size when the overlap in the unmeasured covariates between the studies is small’. That verifies claims 6 and 10 verbatim, and claim 1 follows from the convex-optimization structure of moment balancing. Claims 3, 4, 7, 9 are proposals for software behaviour and reporting, not literature claims. I found no published feasibility-region or support-surface framework for a MAIC sensitivity grid; the closest work assesses positivity violations by simulation rather than returning a feasibility map, so the problem stands open. Claim 5 (trimming or approximate balance changes the estimand) is a correct and uncontroversial consequence of restricting to the overlap population.
GPT-5.6 Sol, technical and source-code lens
open / overstated. The convex-hull claim is correct with an empirical-support qualification. With normalized nonnegative weights, a target moment vector t must satisfy t = sum_i w_i z_i and therefore lie in the convex hull of the source moment vectors. Finite strictly positive exponential tilting generally requires the relative interior; boundary points may require zero weights or a limiting solution. Thus C1, C6, and C10 are sound. C2 and C8 should say that exact reweighting of the observed empirical support is unsupported, not that the target counterfactual itself is logically impossible; finite-sample support and optimizer failure must also be distinguished. C3 is right that software should flag or abstain, but a bound is not automatic and requires additional outcome and selection assumptions. C4 proposes useful diagnostics, although local ESS needs a definition, hull distance depends on scaling and norm, and ordinary outside-hull distance is zero throughout the interior, so distance to the boundary or depth is also needed. C5 is correct when trimming explicitly changes the target to an overlap population. Ad hoc weight truncation may instead bias estimation of the same target without defining a clean replacement estimand. C11 is false for approximate balance alone: approximate balance generally changes the estimator and leaves residual imbalance, but need not change the estimand. C7 and C9 are sound reporting principles when the listed diagnostics apply; divergent transitions are relevant only when a Bayesian component is fitted. Universal claims about software silently discarding failures were not established. This becomes critical when conclusions rely on infeasible or weak-overlap grid points.

How the evidence falls in time

7 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2012 to 2026. The most recent paper to touch this reports progress on it, and every paper calling it open is older.

This problem is recurrent: three or more papers spanning at least eight years assert it independently. Sustained restatement by authors who mostly do not cite each other is the strongest evidence this reading can offer that a gap is real rather than one group’s framing.

It is also being closed in pieces: partial results come from more than one paper in more than one year, each covering a different part.

Does the later work answer the earlier work? Put to an independent reviewer with the older and newer claims side by side, it judged that the later work answers a component of it, leaving a named part (high confidence). The 2022 geometric-feasibility paper answers the core mathematical question and supplies an operational test for whether a proposed MAIC target moment is attainable. It does not turn that test into the requested sensitivity-grid workflow or establish software reporting standards for infeasible and near-boundary cells. What remains: A PAIC-specific sensitivity-grid implementation that identifies and reports feasible, boundary, and infeasible assumed-moment cells alongside effect estimates, with defined handling of failed fits and ESS collapse.
