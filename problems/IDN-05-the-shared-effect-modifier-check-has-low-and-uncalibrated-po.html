The shared effect modifier check has low and uncalibrated power

Identification, transitivity & conditional constancy
Very high priority
Overstated
A published, software-implemented check on the shared effect modifier assumption already exists: Phillippo et al. (doi:10.1177/0272989X221117162) split the common class interaction into treatment-specific interactions one covariate at a time and compare posteriors and model fit, inside a single nine-study network and with no second IPD trial, and multinma implements the hierarchical relaxation this entry proposed through nma(class_interactions = c(‘common’, ‘exchangeable’, ‘independent’)). The check has low and uncharacterized power, which its authors concede, no calibration threshold and no agreed stability metric, and it is least feasible in the small, IPD-poor networks where the assumption does the most identifying work. The restriction is also not the universal mechanism that makes anchored adjustment work; it becomes an identifying restriction in particular class, component and sparse ML-NMR parameterizations, and hierarchical pooling regularizes weak information rather than creating identification.

Statement

A published, software-implemented check on the shared effect modifier assumption already exists: Phillippo et al. (doi:10.1177/0272989X221117162) split the common class interaction into treatment-specific interactions one covariate at a time and compare posteriors and model fit, inside a single nine-study network and with no second IPD trial, and multinma implements the hierarchical relaxation this entry proposed through nma(class_interactions = c(‘common’, ‘exchangeable’, ‘independent’)). The check has low and uncharacterized power, which its authors concede, no calibration threshold and no agreed stability metric, and it is least feasible in the small, IPD-poor networks where the assumption does the most identifying work. The restriction is also not the universal mechanism that makes anchored adjustment work; it becomes an identifying restriction in particular class, component and sparse ML-NMR parameterizations, and hierarchical pooling regularizes weak information rather than creating identification.

Why it is open

Interaction terms are estimated far less precisely than main effects, so the existing check can pass on intervals wide enough to accommodate almost any drift, while apparent discrepancies can come from non-collapsibility, differing covariate ranges or different outcome definitions instead of genuine modifier drift. Nothing states how much interaction stability is enough, so comparing posteriors and model fit returns a judgment rather than a calibrated verdict. The networks where the restriction carries the most identifying weight are the ones with the least data to relax it, and treatment-specific interactions are rarely estimable where one arm contributes only aggregate summaries.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
Phillippo et al. 2023, Validating the Assumptions of Population Adjustment: Application of Multilevel Network Meta-regression to a Network of Treatments for Plaque Psoriasis, Medical Decision Making assesses population-adjustment assumptions inside a nine-study network: splits the common class interaction into treatment-specific interactions one covariate at a time, checks conditional constancy through residual heterogeneity and inconsistency in sufficiently large networks, and reports that the shared-interaction restriction was required to identify the model where a treatment was informed only by aggregate data
multinma R package, nma() argument class_interactions documents common, exchangeable (hierarchically shrunk) and independent treatment-class effect-modifier interactions. Correction, from study IDN-05: "exchangeable" is accepted by the argument but not implemented. In version 0.9.1.9002 (commit 8489bd83) make_nma_formula() aborts with “Exchangeable treatment class interactions (class_interactions = "exchangeable") not yet supported.” Only "common" and "independent" can be fitted, so the relaxation the source proposed as future work remains future work. The separate class_effects argument does support "exchangeable", but that shrinks treatment class effects, not effect-modifier interactions

Probable solution or research direction

The estimator and the diagnostic exist, so the work is characterization: establish the power of the split-interaction check across network sizes and IPD availability, define a stability metric reporting how far interactions can drift before conclusions move rather than a binary verdict, and set a calibration threshold. Use estimated between-trial interaction drift to set priors or sensitivity envelopes on modifier drift, and report how conclusions move as the restriction is relaxed from common to exchangeable to independent, which multinma already supports. Where mechanistic pharmacology or biomarker evidence exists, use it to inform the shrinkage prior instead of defaulting to a common interaction.

Verification

Verdict. Overstated. 2 independent auditors judged the claim as stated too strong. A published, software-implemented check on the shared effect modifier assumption exists (split the common class interaction into treatment-specific interactions and compare posteriors and model fit, as in Phillippo et al. 2023 with multinma’s class_interactions argument), but it has low and uncharacterized power, no calibration threshold, and no agreed stability metric, and it is infeasible in the small, IPD-poor networks where the assumption does the most identifying work.

The source titled this. “Shared effect modification is assumed rather than estimated or tested” The title says shared effect modification is never estimated or tested, but a published within-network check exists. (Its hierarchical relaxation does not ship in multinma: see the correction in the sources table.)

The source said. “Population adjustment commonly assumes that a covariate modifies the effects of different treatments in the same way, although the same modifier may act differently on different treatments. The only empirical check on offer, testing whether modifier interactions estimated in one IPD trial predict the treatment-effect heterogeneity observed in another, is not standardized and is rarely feasible.”

What was wrong with it. The source called cross-trial replication with two IPD trials the only empirical check on offer and proposed hierarchical shrinkage of treatment-specific interactions as future work. Both auditors found the check published in 2023 as a within-network procedure needing no second IPD trial (doi:10.1177/0272989X221117162) and the shrinkage already shipped in multinma’s class_interactions argument, so what remains open is power, calibration and a stability metric rather than the method itself.

Literature and prior-art check
partially-solved / overstated. I read the full text of Phillippo et al. 2023 (Med Decis Making), which the entry does not cite, and it directly contradicts the statement’s ‘only empirical check on offer’. They assess the shared effect modifier assumption inside a single 9-study network with no second IPD trial, by splitting the common class interaction into treatment-specific interactions one covariate at a time and comparing posteriors and DIC, and they report a result (weak evidence against sharing for weight). The entry’s own proposed direction, hierarchical shrinkage of treatment-specific interactions, is already released software: multinma’s nma() takes class_interactions = c(“common”, “exchangeable”, “independent”) (verified in man/nma.Rd), so exchangeable within-class interactions are fittable today. What remains genuinely open is exactly what Phillippo et al. concede: ‘It is likely that this approach to assessing the shared effect modifier assumption has low power, particularly when data are lacking.’ Power, calibration, a stability metric rather than a binary verdict, and the data conditions under which the check is informative are unresolved. So the problem is real but the framing overstates the void by roughly one decade and one R package. Cites 10.1177/0272989X221117162: Provides and applies a standardized within-network empirical check on the shared effect modifier assumption, not requiring two IPD trials. Cites cran:multinma: Implements common, exchangeable (hierarchically shrunk) and independent treatment-class effect-modifier interactions, i.e. the entry’s proposed direction. This is the auditor’s claim as made, and study IDN-05 found it wrong on the exchangeable option: the argument is documented and accepted but aborts as not yet supported in 0.9.1.9002.

partially-solved / overstated. C1 and C2 are correct: treatment-specific modification can differ, and shared conditional interactions do not make marginal odds or hazard ratios population-invariant. Hierarchical pooling is a coherent relaxation but regularizes weak information rather than creating identification. The shared-interaction restriction is not the universal mechanism that makes anchored PAIC work; an anchored analysis targeting the comparator-trial population can transport the IPD trial contrast and subtract the contrast observed in the target trial without assuming identical interactions for every treatment. Shared interactions become identifying restrictions in particular class, component, or sparse ML-NMR parameterizations. The proposed two-trial replication is also not the only empirical check and does not invariably require two complete IPD datasets: treatment-specific interactions can be compared within a mixed network, and sufficiently detailed subgroup aggregate data may support a limited check. Existing within-network relaxation and model comparison partially address the problem; power, calibration, and prior sensitivity remain open. How the evidence falls in time

16 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2013 to 2026. Partial progress is on the record, and later work still calls this open. Both belong in view.

This problem is recurrent: three or more papers spanning at least eight years assert it independently. Sustained restatement by authors who mostly do not cite each other is the strongest evidence this reading can offer that a gap is real rather than one group’s framing.
