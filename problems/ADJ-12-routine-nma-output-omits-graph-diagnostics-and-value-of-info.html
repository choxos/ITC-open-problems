Routine NMA output omits graph diagnostics and value of information

ADJ-12 Overstated Very high priority Promising

Statement

Most of the quantities this entry calls computable but not computed are computed and published. Connectivity and minimal path length between treatments are in Thom et al. (doi:10.1002/jrsm.1329); contrast-specific evidence paths and per-study contribution are in Papakonstantinou et al. (doi:10.12688/f1000research.14770.3) and shipped as netmeta::netcontrib; component estimability has a signal-flow graph in Li, Shih and Tu (doi:10.1002/jrsm.1623), written in answer to the same complaint this entry makes; and precision-based ranking of the next study design is in Nikolakopoulou et al. (doi:10.1002/sim.6608). The development package cpaic, which lives on GitHub and cannot be installed from CRAN, already computes connectedness, subnetworks, bridging components, component-design rank and null space, per-contrast estimability and contrast-specific edge influence. What is genuinely open is that these diagnostics are not routinely reported in NMA outputs, that articulation and minimal cut-set enumeration are still missing, and that there is no value-of-information machinery for deciding which study’s individual data to acquire.

Why it is open

The quantities exist in papers and in scattered implementations but not in standard NMA output, so an analysis can rest entirely on one articulation edge or one study without that dependence appearing anywhere a reviewer would see it. Component networks are worse, because information flows through the component design matrix rather than through visible treatment edges; note also that null-space dimension is a linear-algebra property of that matrix rather than a graph algorithm, and nullity alone does not settle whether a requested contrast is estimable. Acquisition decisions remain informal because expected information gain and the value of individual-data placement cannot come from topology alone: they require a target contrast and population, a sampling model, priors and a decision utility, and no such machinery has been built for evidence networks. Design also cannot substitute for data, since where no evidence exists for the required contrast an optimal design still requires someone to collect it.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
Thom, White, Welton & Lu, Automated methods to test connectedness and quantify indirectness of evidence in network meta-analysis, Res Synth Methods 2019;10:113-124 Graph algorithms for connectivity, indirect connection and minimal path length between treatments, covering the articulation and minimal-bridge questions, with R and Stata code.
Papakonstantinou et al., Estimating the contribution of studies in network meta-analysis: paths, flows and streams, F1000Res 2018;7:610 Contrast-specific paths of evidence and per-study contribution, that is, vulnerability to removal of a study; implemented as netmeta::netcontrib.
Li, Shih & Tu, Graphical evaluation of evidence structure within a component network meta-analysis, Res Synth Methods 2023;14:596-607 Signal-flow graph over components showing which component effects are identifiable and which need more evidence, written in response to the same complaint this entry makes about component networks.
Nikolakopoulou, Mavridis & Salanti, Planning future studies based on the precision of network meta-analysis results, Stat Med 2016;35:219 Ranks candidate future study designs and sample sizes from network structure and precision, covering the design-prioritization half of this entry.
netmeta::netcontrib reference manual Shipped implementation of the contribution matrix in network meta-analysis, evidence that per-study contribution is available to practitioners today.
Heath, Manolopoulou, Baio & Welton, Value of Information for Clinical Trial Design: The Importance of Considering All Relevant Comparators, PharmacoEconomics 2024 Value-of-information trial design that accounts for the full comparator set, the natural starting point for ranking candidate bridging studies by expected decision value.
cpaic connectivity, estimability and edge-influence diagnostics (development package, GitHub commit 9d150e9) Unreleased development package, not installable from CRAN. Implements treatment-network connectedness, subnetworks, bridging components, component-design rank and null space, per-contrast row-space estimability, and a contrast-specific edge-influence diagnostic that reports whether individual data inform the contrast at all.

Probable solution or research direction

Make the existing diagnostics routine rather than rebuilding them: bring connectivity, contribution, component estimability and design-precision tools into standard reporting for treatment and component networks, and add what is genuinely missing, articulation and minimal cut-set enumeration. Build expected-value-of-information methods on top by specifying the target contrast, population, sampling model, priors and decision utility, so candidate new edges, individual-data acquisitions and bridging studies can be scored by their effect on identification and on the decision. Existing value-of-information work for multi-comparator trial design (doi:10.1007/s40273-024-01372-0) is the natural starting point.

Verification

Verdict. Overstated. 2 independent auditors judged the claim as stated too strong. Connectivity, evidence-flow contribution, component estimability graphs and precision-based design of the next trial are all published and partly implemented in netmeta; what is missing is that they are not routinely reported in NMA outputs, and there is no value-of-information machinery for deciding which study’s IPD to acquire.

The source titled this. “Graph and value-of-information diagnostics for network design” The title names graph diagnostics as an open item, but the audit found connectivity, evidence-flow and component-estimability methods published and partly shipped in netmeta.

The source said. “The report lists computable structural quantities that evidence and component networks should report (articulation treatments and components, minimal bridge sets, null-space dimension, contrast-specific paths of component information, vulnerability to removal of a study or component, centrality of IPD placement, expected information gain from adding an edge) and proposes Bayesian experimental design and active learning to rank which IPD to acquire or which bridging trial to run.”

What was wrong with it. The source listed articulation treatments, minimal bridge sets, null-space dimension, contrast-specific information paths and vulnerability to study removal as computable but not computed. The literature auditor found four published methods covering most of them, one of which ships in netmeta, and GPT-5.6 Sol found rank, null space, bridging components, per-contrast estimability and edge influence already implemented in the unreleased cpaic development package; what survives is routine reporting and value of information for individual-data acquisition.

Dissent. grok dissented (supported-with-caveat): Pieces of the agenda already exist; the integrated reporting-and-design stack does not. Value-of-information methods for multi-comparator / network-aware research prioritization and trial design are active (e.g. Heath, Manolopoulou, Baio, Welton, PharmacoEconomics 2024 on VOI when all relevant comparators matter; longer Welton/Ades-style EVSI tradition). That partially substantiates ranking candidate bridging trials or evidence acquisitions by expected information for decision. Graph algorithms can in principle compute articulation points, bridges, and related connectivity; NMA practice already has leave-one-out / influence style checks and geometry plots (netmeta and CINeMA ecosystem). What remains open is the report’s full computable suite as standard output: articulation treatments and components, minimal bridge sets, component-design-matrix null-space dimension, contrast-specific component information paths, IPD-placement centrality, and design scores tied to those quantities. I found no 2024–2026 package release or merged PR that implements that diagnostic battery for treatment and component networks as routine reporting. Design still cannot invent missing likelihood for a contrast.

The findings overwhelmingly establish poor routine reporting and incomplete uptake, not that connectivity, contribution, estimability, or next-study design methods are unavailable. GPT-5.6 Sol, technical and source-code lens
partially-solved / overstated. C1 is straightforward for articulation treatments, but an articulation component needs an explicit bipartite or hypergraph definition and is not equivalent to statistical estimability. C2 and structural leave-one-out versions of C6 are computable once the bridge set is defined. C3 is a design-matrix linear-algebra calculation, not a graph algorithm, and nullity alone does not determine a requested contrast. C4 requires a model and weighting rule and can be non-unique in cyclic or rank-deficient networks. C5 and C7 cannot be obtained from topology alone: expected information gain and the inferential value of IPD require a target contrast and population, sampling model, sample size, overlap, priors, and usually a decision utility. C8 through C10 are sound with those ingredients, and C11 reports the source label. At cpaic commit 9d150e9, cpaic_connectivity already returns connectedness, subnetworks, bridging components, rank, null space, and per-contrast estimability, while edge_influence reports contrast-specific edge weights and whether IPD placement informs the contrast. Thus the blanket claim that the quantities are not computed is false, although articulation or cut-set enumeration, removal vulnerability, and model-based value-of-information design remain open. Very high is justified for estimability and fragility diagnostics, not for the entire active-learning bundle. How the evidence falls in time

24 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2013 to 2024. Partial progress is on the record, and later work still calls this open. Both belong in view.

This problem is recurrent: three or more papers spanning at least eight years assert it independently. Sustained restatement by authors who mostly do not cite each other is the strongest evidence this reading can offer that a gap is real rather than one group’s framing.

It is also being closed in pieces: partial results come from more than one paper in more than one year, each covering a different part.
