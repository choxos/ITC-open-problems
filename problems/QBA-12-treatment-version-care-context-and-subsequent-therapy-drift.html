Treatment version, care context and subsequent-therapy drift

Quantitative bias analysis for unanchored PAIC
High priority
Confirmed open
Differences in dose, backbone therapy, rescue and subsequent therapy, treatment switching, monitoring intensity and calendar era between the compared studies are a real threat, because with one nominal treatment per study baseline patient balancing cannot separate a study-level implementation shift from the treatment effect and cannot standardize post-baseline therapy. Whether they constitute bias at all depends on the estimand: these differences may define different treatment versions, and post-progression therapy and switching may belong to a treatment-policy estimand rather than being bias in a common treatment effect. Study-level or era-level treatment-shift parameters, hierarchical drift models and commensurate priors can encode assumptions where replicated studies supply the structure, but none is universally required and none identifies perfectly confounded drift on its own; g-methods, intercurrent-event estimands and partial identification are alternatives. The same drift arises in the comparator arm of a connected network, not only in an unanchored comparison: where the care actually delivered under a nominally common control differs across trials, in supervision intensity, monitoring schedule or co-delivered attention, the anchor stops canceling study-level effects, and the analyst’s only options are to keep a contaminated anchor or to discard the comparator arm and accept every unmeasured prognostic difference in an unanchored analysis. No estimator occupies the middle by modeling the control-arm discrepancy explicitly, for instance through a study-level care-intensity or control-response parameter, and no diagnostic states how contaminated an anchor must be before dropping it is the better trade.

QBA-12 Confirmed open High priority Emerging

Statement

Differences in dose, backbone therapy, rescue and subsequent therapy, treatment switching, monitoring intensity and calendar era between the compared studies are a real threat, because with one nominal treatment per study baseline patient balancing cannot separate a study-level implementation shift from the treatment effect and cannot standardize post-baseline therapy. Whether they constitute bias at all depends on the estimand: these differences may define different treatment versions, and post-progression therapy and switching may belong to a treatment-policy estimand rather than being bias in a common treatment effect. Study-level or era-level treatment-shift parameters, hierarchical drift models and commensurate priors can encode assumptions where replicated studies supply the structure, but none is universally required and none identifies perfectly confounded drift on its own; g-methods, intercurrent-event estimands and partial identification are alternatives. The same drift arises in the comparator arm of a connected network, not only in an unanchored comparison: where the care actually delivered under a nominally common control differs across trials, in supervision intensity, monitoring schedule or co-delivered attention, the anchor stops canceling study-level effects, and the analyst’s only options are to keep a contaminated anchor or to discard the comparator arm and accept every unmeasured prognostic difference in an unanchored analysis. No estimator occupies the middle by modeling the control-arm discrepancy explicitly, for instance through a study-level care-intensity or control-response parameter, and no diagnostic states how contaminated an anchor must be before dropping it is the better trade.

Why it is open

In an unanchored comparison the treatment and the study are the same unit, so any difference in how the treatment was delivered is indistinguishable from a treatment effect, and covariate balancing cannot address drift that is a property of the protocol and care pathway rather than of patients. Switching and subsequent-therapy differences act after baseline, so they operate through a post-randomization pathway that baseline balancing cannot touch, and they change which estimand the comparison targets, because a treatment-policy survival estimate under one set of rescue options is not comparable to one under another. Encoding drift requires study-level design covariates and subsequent-therapy patterns that are seldom extracted, usually only narratively described in the comparator publication and never balanced, and no published PAIC method implements the hierarchical or commensurate structures that would let those assumptions be varied.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
Phillippo, Ades, Dias, Palmer, Abrams and Welton 2018, Methods for Population-Adjusted Indirect Comparisons in Health Technology Appraisal, Medical Decision Making (the peer-reviewed account of NICE DSU TSD 18) States that unanchored comparisons make much stronger assumptions, widely regarded as infeasible, and that the accuracy of an unanchored estimate is unknown because residual bias from unobserved factors cannot be quantified. Supports the structural claims but implements none of the drift parameterizations the entry proposes.
Cooper, Kilvert, Hodgkins, Roskell and Eldar-Lissai 2019, CNS Drugs A worked case of care-context drift inside a nominally common comparator: the pooled brexanolone trials’ placebo arm delivered 60 hours of medically supervised inpatient infusion care plus regular observation, against outpatient oral placebo elsewhere, and the placebo-arm improvement was substantially larger and faster than in the comparator trials. The response was to judge the arm unsuitable as a common comparator, discard it, and run unanchored matching-adjusted comparisons against other trials’ placebo arms, then rebuild the indirect comparison from those. Anchored and anchor-discarded results are reported side by side, with the stated conclusion that a simple network meta-analysis can give misleading results here, and with the concessions that once the placebo arm is discarded there is no common comparator, that unanchored analyses are more likely to be biased, and that only a small number of relevant characteristics were available for matching. This documents the binary choice the entry describes, keep a contaminated anchor or abandon randomization, and shows nothing measures the trade.
Achana, Cooper, Dias, Lu, Rice, Kendrick and Sutton 2013, Statistics in Medicine Extends baseline-risk meta-regression from pairwise meta-analysis to networks, giving a study-level control-response structure that a middle-ground estimator could use to absorb comparator-arm discrepancy while retaining the randomized within-study contrast; it is the closest available machinery and has not been connected to the anchored versus unanchored decision.
Dias, Welton, Sutton and Ades 2013, Medical Decision Making (NICE DSU TSD 5) The baseline natural history model, which synthesizes the control-arm response separately from the relative effects; the existing device for representing a comparator arm whose absolute performance differs across studies, again not linked to the decision of whether an anchor should be kept.

Probable solution or research direction

State the target intervention and the intercurrent-event strategy first, so it is explicit whether an observed delivery difference is a different treatment version, part of a treatment-policy estimand, or a departure to be modeled. Where it is a departure, introduce study-level or era-level treatment-shift parameters and report results across a range of assumed drift magnitudes rather than assuming exact treatment-version equivalence, using hierarchical drift models or commensurate priors only where replicated studies give their identifying structure some purchase, and g-methods or partial identification otherwise. Analyze these parameters jointly with the confounding and censoring parameters rather than as disconnected scenario analyses.

Verification

Verdict. Confirmed open. Auditors agreed the problem is open and no counterevidence surfaced.

The source said. “Differences in dose, backbone therapy, rescue and subsequent therapy, treatment switching, monitoring intensity and calendar era between the compared studies form a distinct bias target requiring study-specific or era-specific treatment-shift parameters, handled with design-bias parameters, hierarchical drift models or commensurate priors.”

What was wrong with it. The entry named one class of sensitivity parameters and analysis families as the appropriate response; GPT-5.6 Sol found that too prescriptive, since the differences may define a different treatment version or belong to a treatment-policy estimand, and hierarchical drift models and commensurate priors cannot identify drift that is perfectly confounded with study. The literature auditor could verify none of the entry’s proposals against any published PAIC method and voted unverifiable on support; the entry cited nothing, and the structural point that residual bias from unobserved factors in unanchored comparisons cannot be quantified is in Phillippo et al. 2017 (doi:10.1177/0272989X17725740), the peer-reviewed account of NICE DSU TSD 18.

Literature and prior-art check
open / unverifiable. This entry has no prior_work and all five claims are normative or definitional. Claims 3 and 5 are logically true given the unanchored design (treatment and study are the same unit; baseline balancing cannot act on post-baseline subsequent therapy) and are consistent with NICE DSU TSD 18 and with Phillippo et al., who note that residual bias from unobserved factors in unanchored comparisons cannot be quantified. Claims 1, 2 and 4 assert that a specific class of sensitivity parameters and analysis families (study- or era-specific treatment-shift parameters, hierarchical drift models, commensurate priors) is the appropriate response; that is a proposal, not a verifiable statement about the literature, and I found no published PAIC method implementing it. Commensurate priors and hierarchical drift models are established devices in historical-borrowing and bias-adjustment literature (bias-adjusted meta-analysis, NICE DSU bias-adjustment TSDs), so the components exist even though the PAIC assembly does not. I vote unverifiable on support rather than guess.

How the evidence falls in time

This problem is recurrent: three or more papers spanning at least eight years assert it independently. Sustained restatement by authors who mostly do not cite each other is the strongest evidence this reading can offer that a gap is real rather than one group’s framing.
