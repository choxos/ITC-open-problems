No agreed leave-one-out unit for mixed IPD and aggregate likelihoods

Software, benchmarks & interface standards
High priority
Partially addressed
Open multinma issues record poor Pareto-k behavior together with ambiguity over the unit of leave-one-out prediction (#60), absent prior-sensitivity tooling (#32) and uncertainty around rankings and SUCRA (#17). Two of these are implementation gaps with published prescriptions rather than missing methods: #32 asks multinma to expose log-prior evaluations so the existing power-scaling method in priorsense can be used, and #17 asks for SUCRA credible intervals following Trinquart et al., which is arithmetic on the mean-rank posterior. multinma 0.9.1 already reports posterior rank distributions with credible intervals through posterior_ranks() and shows prior-to-posterior movement through plot_prior_posterior(), so the ranking and prior-dependence gaps are narrower than stated. What is genuinely unsettled is the held-out unit: loo.stan_nma delegates to rstan::loo while the log-likelihood mixes patient-level IPD, arm-level aggregate and study-level contrast contributions, so the unit is heterogeneous, no unit selection or exact-refit fallback is exposed, and no field convention fixes which unit answers which predictive question.

Statement

Open multinma issues record poor Pareto-k behavior together with ambiguity over the unit of leave-one-out prediction (#60), absent prior-sensitivity tooling (#32) and uncertainty around rankings and SUCRA (#17). Two of these are implementation gaps with published prescriptions rather than missing methods: #32 asks multinma to expose log-prior evaluations so the existing power-scaling method in priorsense can be used, and #17 asks for SUCRA credible intervals following Trinquart et al., which is arithmetic on the mean-rank posterior. multinma 0.9.1 already reports posterior rank distributions with credible intervals through posterior_ranks() and shows prior-to-posterior movement through plot_prior_posterior(), so the ranking and prior-dependence gaps are narrower than stated. What is genuinely unsettled is the held-out unit: loo.stan_nma delegates to rstan::loo while the log-likelihood mixes patient-level IPD, arm-level aggregate and study-level contrast contributions, so the unit is heterogeneous, no unit selection or exact-refit fallback is exposed, and no field convention fixes which unit answers which predictive question.

Why it is open

In a hierarchical evidence-synthesis model there is no single natural held-out unit, and multinma’s log-likelihood mixes patients, arms and study-level contrasts, so a reported leave-one-out number aggregates over units that answer different predictive questions and is not comparable across analyses. No convention fixes the unit and no exact-refit or grouped fallback is exposed for when the importance-sampling approximation degrades, which is precisely when the model is complex enough for the comparison to matter. Prior dependence in sparse or partially disconnected networks is visible only if the analyst chooses to look, since power-scaling needs a log-prior the package does not expose and the prior-versus-posterior plot is qualitative. Rankings compound this because SUCRA is a nonlinear functional of the posterior reported without an interval, even though the mean-rank interval it would be derived from is already available.

Probable solution or research direction

Settle the held-out unit first, because it is the only item with no published answer: define what leaving out a patient, an arm, a study or a treatment means in hierarchical NMA and ML-NMR, implement a diagnostic for each, and provide an exact-refit or grouped fallback when Pareto-k diagnostics fail. The other three are plumbing against known prescriptions: expose log-prior evaluations so priorsense power-scaling works, derive SUCRA intervals from the mean-rank posterior as Trinquart et al. specify, and surface both by default rather than as optional extra analysis. Report Pareto-k failure as a statement about the approximation rather than about the model, so users do not discard a valid fit.

Verification

Verdict. Partially addressed. Part of this problem is addressed by existing work; the entry names which part.

The source titled this. “No agreed leave-one-out unit or prior-sensitivity tooling for ML-NMR” The title says prior-sensitivity tooling is absent, but the power-scaling prescription is off the shelf and prior-posterior plotting already ships in multinma 0.9.1.

The source said. “Open multinma issues record poor Pareto-k behavior, ambiguity over the unit of leave-one-out prediction, absent prior-sensitivity tooling and unquantified uncertainty around rankings and SUCRA, alongside a table caution that predictive validation for this class of model is complex. These are model-validation and decision-uncertainty problems for hierarchical evidence synthesis, not only diagnostic warnings in one package.”

What was wrong with it. Both auditors narrowed the entry: the literature auditor found that prior-sensitivity and SUCRA uncertainty have off-the-shelf prescriptions named in the issues themselves (priorsense power-scaling in #32, Trinquart et al. in #17), so no tooling exists overstates them, and GPT-5.6 Sol found posterior_ranks() and plot_prior_posterior() already shipping in 0.9.1. GPT-5.6 Sol also corrected the reading of Pareto-k, which signals that the importance-sampling approximation is unreliable rather than that the fitted model has failed.

How the evidence falls in time

8 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2014 to 2022. The most recent paper to touch this reports progress on it, and every paper calling it open is older.

It is also being closed in pieces: partial results come from more than one paper in more than one year, each covering a different part.

Does the later work answer the earlier work? Put to an independent reviewer with the older and newer claims side by side, it judged that the later work answers a component of it, leaving a named part (medium confidence). The later work establishes general principles for choosing grouped LOO units and provides a trial-level solution for pure aggregate-data meta-analysis. Neither resolves how mixed-granularity likelihood contributions should be grouped or compared in an ITC/PAIC implementation such as multinma. What remains: An agreed held-out unit and predictive estimand for likelihoods combining patient-level IPD, aggregate-arm, and study-level contrast contributions, with suitable diagnostics and exact-refit fallback.
