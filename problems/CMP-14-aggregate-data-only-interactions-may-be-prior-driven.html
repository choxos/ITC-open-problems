Aggregate-data-only interactions may be prior-driven

Component methods (CNMA and component PAIC)
Very high priority
Partially addressed
Interaction parameters informed only by aggregate data can be regularized into finite posterior intervals by the prior, so a proper credible interval is not evidence that the likelihood contributed anything. Implemented component PAIC already ships partial mitigation: plot_prior_posterior() compares each prior with its posterior, prior_sensitivity() quantifies how far target contrasts move when priors are tightened or loosened, and estimable_effects_at() separates no first-order information from IPD-supported and aggregate-only information. What is missing is a default numerical summary: prior-to-posterior contraction per interaction parameter and an effective likelihood rank, reported without the analyst having to ask.

Statement

Interaction parameters informed only by aggregate data can be regularized into finite posterior intervals by the prior, so a proper credible interval is not evidence that the likelihood contributed anything. Implemented component PAIC already ships partial mitigation: plot_prior_posterior() compares each prior with its posterior, prior_sensitivity() quantifies how far target contrasts move when priors are tightened or loosened, and estimable_effects_at() separates no first-order information from IPD-supported and aggregate-only information. What is missing is a default numerical summary: prior-to-posterior contraction per interaction parameter and an effective likelihood rank, reported without the analyst having to ask.

Why it is open

A proper prior yields a proper posterior under ordinary integrability conditions, so the usual output gives no signal that the likelihood contributed nothing, and the parameters where this bites hardest are exactly the weakly identified interaction terms that a bridged component model depends on. The existing diagnostics are visual or require a deliberate refit, so an analyst who does not run them sees a clean interval with no warning attached. Prior-to-posterior contraction and effective likelihood rank are the summaries that would make this visible by default, and neither is computed.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
cpaic 0.1.0 (development version, choxos/cpaic@9d150e9) unreleased development R package on GitHub, not on CRAN; records prior specifications, provides plot_prior_posterior(), prior_sensitivity(), and estimable_effects_at(), documents that weakly identified interaction parameters are regularized by priors, and reports no numerical prior-to-posterior contraction or effective likelihood rank

Probable solution or research direction

Promote the existing prior-versus-posterior comparison and prior-sensitivity refit from optional diagnostics to default reported output, and add numerical prior-to-posterior contraction for every interaction parameter plus an effective likelihood rank for the fitted model. Attach to each reported interaction the label of whether it is identified by IPD, by aggregate arms, or by neither, which the estimability screen already computes. Calibrate these summaries specifically to interaction parameters in a bridged component model, since the generic Bayesian workflow versions are already standard.

Our study

Do prior-sensitivity diagnostics catch the analyses that are actually wrong?

This study was designed against CMU-02 and bears on this entry in part. What it does not answer is stated below.

The question put to it. In a population-adjusted network a proper prior yields a proper posterior even where the likelihood identifies nothing, so a narrow interval can reflect the prior rather than the evidence. The diagnostics that would expose this exist. What are their operating characteristics: do they flag the analyses whose intervals actually miss the truth, and how often do they cry wolf?

What it found. At the thresholds the literature suggests, they miss most of it. Pairing each warning with whether that replicate’s own interval missed, a composite of four diagnostics has sensitivity 0.37 at a false-alarm rate of 0.16, falling to 0.13 outside two engineered geometries; the one sensitive rule fires on half the sound analyses too. Used as continuous scores the same statistics reach AUC 0.51 to 0.78, so this is a failure of thresholds rather than of the statistics. What no threshold repairs is that these diagnostics measure the prior’s influence while the harm comes from the prior’s location: with disconnected evidence and a tight prior four standard deviations from the truth, going from 100 to 400 per arm drops the warning rate from 0.98 to 0.01 while coverage stays near zero.

Results.

  • Prespecified verdict: diagnostics fail at the thresholds the literature suggests. Composite sensitivity 0.366 (MCSE 0.002), false alarm 0.158; 0.127 and 0.025 outside the engineered positive controls.
  • Sensitivity and specificity trade off completely. Tight-and-loose prior refitting catches 0.83 of misses outside the controls and fires on 53% of covered replicates. Contraction, the prior-only benchmark and power-scaling keep false alarms low and catch almost nothing.
  • The composite adds almost nothing over its best component: 0.366 against contraction’s 0.363, at a higher false-alarm rate. Requiring two of four rules to agree neither buys specificity nor recovers sensitivity.
  • More data makes the warning quieter while the answer stays wrong. Disconnected evidence, tight prior, true interaction four prior SDs away: coverage near zero at both sample sizes, warning rate 0.98 at 100 per arm and 0.01 at 400. Contraction is what every diagnostic reads as reassurance.
  • A first implementation measured power-scaling by posterior mean shift and found sensitivity 0.000 everywhere. That was our error: with a zero-centred prior, scaling the prior up and the likelihood down give identical posterior means, so the standard rule can never fire. Measured distributionally it works, and the verdict does not depend on the amendment.
  • The structural rank screen is the only rule with no false alarms at all, and it is blind to every failure where the contrast is estimable and the answer is still wrong.

Design.

Estimand
Target-population marginal C versus B mean difference, and the conditional interaction coefficient
Methods compared
no-diagnostic baseline, structural rank screen, prior-to-posterior contraction, prior-only benchmark, power-scaling sensitivity, tight and loose refit, composite rule
Data-generating mechanism
A conjugate Gaussian population-adjusted network in which every piece of evidence reduces without loss to a normal observation, so the posterior and every power-scaled posterior are exact and no diagnostic is contaminated by MCMC noise. 120 scenarios crossing the evidence available about the weakly identified treatment, arm size, whether the truth agrees with the zero-centered prior, prior scale, and the target population. 2000 replicates each.
Replicates
2000 per scenario, 240,000 datasets and 480,000 contrast evaluations

What this does not answer. The other half of CMU-02, wiring these diagnostics into released software, is untouched; neither multinma nor cpaic is run. The model is conjugate Gaussian with an identity link, a correctly specified linear mean and known variances, so nothing transfers directly to a non-conjugate posterior where MCMC error enters and the diagnostics behave differently. Only automated thresholds are evaluated; a plot read by an experienced analyst is a different instrument. Thresholds were taken from the source literature and not tuned. CMP-14 is touched only for the generic aggregate-only interaction, not for component models, and IDN-06 is not addressed at all because this design contains no within-versus-between discrepancy.

Peer review. 2 rounds, two independent reviewers. The reports, the point-by-point responses and the editorial decision are published in full: read the review.

Verification

Verdict. Partially addressed. Part of this problem is addressed by existing work; the entry names which part.

The source said. “Interaction parameters informed only by aggregate data can be regularized into finite posterior intervals by the prior rather than by the likelihood. A finite credible interval may therefore reflect the prior rather than information in the data.”

What was wrong with it. The source said no diagnostic in standard output separates prior-driven from likelihood-driven interaction estimates. GPT-5.6 Sol found three at choxos/cpaic@9d150e9 (plot_prior_posterior(), prior_sensitivity(), estimable_effects_at()), so the gap narrows to their being optional rather than default and to the absence of numerical contraction and likelihood-rank summaries; it also corrected the wording, since a prior regularizes a posterior but never makes the likelihood identify a parameter.

Literature and prior-art check
open / supported-with-caveat. The statistical content is uncontroversial Bayesian bookkeeping: a proper prior always yields a proper posterior, so a finite credible interval is not evidence of likelihood information. I found no paper offering a routine prior-versus-likelihood diagnostic for effect-modifier interactions in ML-NMR or CNMA, and no source asserting that absence, so the ‘no diagnostic in standard output’ claims are unverifiable rather than established. Two caveats for the editor. The generic remedies named (prior-to-posterior contraction, prior predictive checks, prior sensitivity) are standard Bayesian workflow rather than a research gap, so the novel part is specifically calibrating them to interaction parameters in a bridged component model. And the claim about what cpaic’s documentation recognises cannot be checked: cpaic is not on CRAN and I found no public repository. Confidence is capped because I read no text bearing directly on this entry.
GPT-5.6 Sol, technical and source-code lens
partially-solved / supported-with-caveat. C2-C4 are supported: a proper posterior interval can be dominated by prior regularization, cpaic has no numerical prior-to-posterior contraction table or effective-likelihood-rank report, and its documentation explicitly recognizes prior regularization of weakly identified Gamma. C1 should say prior-regularized or prior-driven posterior rather than likelihood identification: a prior does not make the likelihood identify a parameter. Proper-prior/proper-posterior language also requires ordinary posterior-integrability conditions rather than holding as an unrestricted theorem. The report understates existing mitigation: plot_prior_posterior() directly compares each prior and posterior, prior_sensitivity() quantifies contrast movement under changed priors, and estimable_effects_at() distinguishes IPD, aggregate, and absent first-order information. These are optional rather than default numerical contraction or likelihood-rank summaries, so the remaining work is useful but is a diagnostic-roadmap item, not automatically a very-high-severity correctness failure.
