Evidence-set assembly can favor the sponsor before modeling begins

Evidence-base construction & data access
Very high priority
Overstated
Eligibility and comparator decisions taken while assembling the evidence base can materially change an indirect comparison by changing which studies, populations and contrasts are represented, and this is demonstrated rather than hypothetical: a multiverse of 9216 indirect comparison meta-analyses of nalmefene versus naltrexone reported a Janus effect (Palpacuer et al. 2019, doi:10.1186/s12916-019-1409-3), and a multiverse over all possible antidepressant networks found 172 of 231 pairwise comparisons showing opposite effects between the 1st and 99th percentiles (Vinatier et al. 2024, doi:10.1136/bmjebm-2024-112848). What remains open is that multiverse reporting is not required or routinely performed in sponsor submissions, and there is no accepted criterion for which alternative networks count as defensible. Selection changes how relevant the assembled evidence is to an externally defined target population; it does not by itself define the target estimand. The same discretion operates on the boundary of the network and not only on which studies pass eligibility. Admitting a non-focal treatment can connect the network, sharpen the focal contrasts and stabilize the heterogeneity estimate while importing populations whose effect-modifier distributions threaten transitivity, and because a rank is defined relative to the treatments in the network, scope is part of the estimand rather than a nuisance choice. No criterion prices that trade-off in a common currency, the search and extraction cost of extension falls on the reviewer rather than on the decision maker, and only 40 percent of 365 published drug network meta-analyses reported any objective criterion for which drugs or classes were admitted. Ranking output is affected more sharply than effect estimates, because SUCRA and P-score are functionals of the whole admitted node set, so admitting regimens that no decision-maker can choose shifts every other treatment’s position in the hierarchy. Nothing in current reporting requires the node-admission rule to be stated, requires the hierarchy to be recomputed over a decision-relevant set of available and viable regimens, or reports any measure of how far a treatment’s rank depends on inadmissible nodes.

EVB-02 Overstated Very high priority Emerging

Statement

Eligibility and comparator decisions taken while assembling the evidence base can materially change an indirect comparison by changing which studies, populations and contrasts are represented, and this is demonstrated rather than hypothetical: a multiverse of 9216 indirect comparison meta-analyses of nalmefene versus naltrexone reported a Janus effect (Palpacuer et al. 2019, doi:10.1186/s12916-019-1409-3), and a multiverse over all possible antidepressant networks found 172 of 231 pairwise comparisons showing opposite effects between the 1st and 99th percentiles (Vinatier et al. 2024, doi:10.1136/bmjebm-2024-112848). Selection changes how relevant the assembled evidence is to an externally defined target population; it does not by itself define the target estimand. The same discretion operates on the boundary of the network and not only on which studies pass eligibility. Admitting a non-focal treatment can connect the network, sharpen the focal contrasts and stabilize the heterogeneity estimate while importing populations whose effect-modifier distributions threaten transitivity, and because a rank is defined relative to the treatments in the network, scope is part of the estimand rather than a nuisance choice. Nothing in current reporting requires the node-admission rule to be stated, requires the hierarchy to be recomputed over a decision-relevant set of available and viable regimens, or reports any measure of how far a treatment’s rank depends on inadmissible nodes.

Why it is open

The evidence set is assembled by the party that reports the result, and every downstream analysis conditions on the assembled network, so no covariate adjustment recovers the comparisons that were excluded. The tools to expose this now exist and their published applications show inclusion choices can flip the direction of an estimate, but nothing obliges a sponsor submission to report one, and the demonstrations to date sit in the general synthesis literature rather than in HTA practice. An undefined multiverse over all defensible networks also reintroduces analyst discretion and multiplicity, so the missing piece is a transparent rule for what counts as defensible rather than the technique itself.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
Vinatier et al. 2024, BMJ Evidence-Based Medicine Multiverse analysis covering all possible network meta-analyses over treatment combinations across 522 antidepressant trials and 116,477 patients, quantifying how far conclusions move with the assembly step.
Palpacuer et al. 2019, BMC Medicine Enumerates 9216 indirect comparison meta-analyses of nalmefene versus naltrexone by varying eligibility criteria and analytic model, showing that inclusion decisions alone can reverse the direction of the comparison.
Guelimi et al. 2025, BMC Medical Research Methodology Quantifies how inclusion criteria and other methodological choices change network meta-analysis conclusions in psoriasis; shows sensitivity over alternative networks is operational.
Tonin et al. 2018, PLOS ONE Maps 365 drug network meta-analyses and finds that fewer than half, 146 or 40 percent, reported objective criteria for selecting the drugs or classes included, while 87 or 23.8 percent gave explicitly non-objective reasons such as most commonly used drugs, with no improvement over the period studied. Supplies the base rate for undisclosed node-admission rules.
Batson et al. 2016, PLOS ONE Extends an atrial fibrillation network from 4 trials to 19 trials and 15 treatments solely to make covariate and baseline-risk models fittable, finds no interaction signal, and states that there is an unmet practical need to know how far to extend a network in the search for indirect evidence in health technology assessment.
Caldwell et al. 2015, Value in Health Supplies the precision half of the scope decision and concludes that reviewers should include only those additional non-focal treatments compared with at least two focal treatments. Names value-of-information analysis as the tool for the cost and bias halves without applying it, and leaves the bias consequence of extension as an important question to address.
Caldwell 2014, Systematic Reviews Overview of conducting systematic reviews with network meta-analysis, which records that network size has been called an unsolved issue in network meta-analysis and an area of developing interest, and reduces practice to prespecifying a population, intervention, comparator and outcome plus an extension strategy.
Babul et al. 2026, Cureus Editorial examining how dose and regimen nodes were admitted into three alopecia areata evidence syntheses of oral JAK inhibitors. In a network of placebo against 22 active interventions, only six corresponded to approved drugs at approved doses, ritlecitinib entered at five regimens although one is approved, and deuruxolitinib 12 mg twice daily, never approved and placed under an FDA clinical hold in May 2023 for unfavorable benefit-risk, was reported in the abstract as the most efficacious intervention for both the primary and the aspirational endpoint. Its remedy is procedural: prespecify exclusion of doses never advanced to pivotal trials or analyze them only in prespecified sensitivity analyses, and report approval status and regulatory actions alongside pooled estimates.

Probable solution or research direction

The multiverse machinery exists and the work is to require and discipline it rather than to invent it: specify transparent rules for which alternative evidence sets are defensible, then report the estimate across them as a matter of course. Protocol-first feasibility assessment that fixes eligibility before results are known addresses the outcome-aware part of the problem that a multiverse cannot, since a multiverse run after the fact still starts from a chosen frame. Applying target-trial emulation to the construction of the evidence set rather than to a single analysis remains unevidenced; the literature auditor could not verify any published instance and Grok also left it open.

Verification

Verdict. Overstated. 2 independent auditors judged the claim as stated too strong. Multiverse analysis over the assembled evidence set has been demonstrated for indirect comparisons and for network meta-analysis and shows that inclusion decisions can flip the direction of the estimate; what remains open is that it is not routinely required or performed in sponsor submissions, and there is no accepted criterion for which alternative networks count as defensible.

The source said. “Eligibility and comparator decisions taken while assembling the evidence base can induce a favorable network or a favorable target population before any modeling begins, and only one of the defensible networks is ever shown.”

What was wrong with it. The source presented multiverse analysis over defensible networks as an emerging direction and said alternative networks are never reported; the literature auditor found two published multiverses of exactly that object (Palpacuer et al. 2019, doi:10.1186/s12916-019-1409-3; Vinatier et al. 2024, doi:10.1136/bmjebm-2024-112848), with a third for inclusion criteria in psoriasis (Guelimi et al. 2025, doi:10.1186/s12874-025-02558-6). GPT-5.6 Sol separately rejected the conflation of the population represented by the selected studies with an externally defined target population, and struck the universal claims that every inclusion decision is outcome-aware and that alternatives are never shown.

Dissent. grok dissented (supported-with-caveat): The claim that eligibility and comparator choices can favor a network or target population before modeling, and that only one defensible network is typically shown, remains faithful for HTA/ITC practice. Recent work does, however, implement the multiverse response for treatment/network assembly in evidence synthesis, so the problem is not open as if no method existed. Vinatier et al. (2024) ran a multiverse covering all possible NMAs on different treatment combinations in antidepressants and showed large vibration of effects, especially for indirect comparisons. Guelimi et al. (2025) quantified how inclusion criteria and other methodological choices change NMA conclusions in psoriasis and explicitly contrasted their design with prior all-possible-networks work. These papers substantiate C1/C4 as live, demonstrated issues and give a usable multiverse template. They do not solve sponsor-side selective assembly, protocol-first eligibility fixed before knowing results, or target-trial emulation applied to the evidence set itself (C3); those remain open. RoB-NMA (2025) and PRISMA-NMA update work improve transparency of synthesis bias but do not replace multiverse reporting of defensible networks.

open / overstated. Evidence-set selection can materially change network connectivity, available contrasts, precision, and conclusions, so C1 and the underlying robustness problem are valid. C2 conflates the population represented by the selected studies with the externally defined target population; study selection changes evidential relevance to a target but should not itself define the target estimand. Target-trial principles can help prespecify eligibility and estimand attributes, but calling evidence-set assembly target-trial emulation does not recover excluded information or identify selection effects. C4 is a reasonable sensitivity strategy if defensible networks are specified by transparent rules, although an undefined multiverse of all defensible networks invites further researcher discretion and multiplicity. The assertions that every inclusion decision is outcome-aware and that alternatives are usually or never reported are unsupported universal claims. This can be critical when eligibility was selected after results were known, but it is not automatically a modeling failure in every ITC.

How the evidence falls in time

52 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2010 to 2026. Partial progress is on the record, and later work still calls this open. Both belong in view.

This problem is recurrent: three or more papers spanning at least eight years assert it independently. Sustained restatement by authors who mostly do not cite each other is the strongest evidence this reading can offer that a gap is real rather than one group’s framing.

It is also being closed in pieces: partial results come from more than one paper in more than one year, each covering a different part.
