No reporting standard covers what population adjustment needs

Evidence-base construction & data access
High priority
Unverifiable
Generic baseline-covariate reporting is already mandated: CONSORT 2025 item 25 requires a table of baseline demographic and clinical characteristics for each group, and CONSORT presents itself as a minimum set of items for a trial report (doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1004587). What no standard specifies is what population adjustment needs: which covariates, dispersion measures, any joint or correlation structure, and reporting on the analysis set that matches the reported effect estimate. In practice the adjustment set is therefore bounded by what the comparator publication happened to print; a review of 18 NICE technology appraisals using population adjustment lists availability among the bases on which covariates were selected (Phillippo et al. 2019, doi:10.1017/S0266462319000333).

Statement

Generic baseline-covariate reporting is already mandated: CONSORT 2025 item 25 requires a table of baseline demographic and clinical characteristics for each group, and CONSORT presents itself as a minimum set of items for a trial report (doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1004587). What no standard specifies is what population adjustment needs: which covariates, dispersion measures, any joint or correlation structure, and reporting on the analysis set that matches the reported effect estimate. In practice the adjustment set is therefore bounded by what the comparator publication happened to print; a review of 18 NICE technology appraisals using population adjustment lists availability among the bases on which covariates were selected (Phillippo et al. 2019, doi:10.1017/S0266462319000333).

Why it is open

The analyst does not control the comparator publication, so the moments needed for balancing are supplied or withheld by a third party with no stake in the later comparison. A CONSORT-style baseline table does not close the gap, because what is required varies with the estimand and the model, and marginal means and proportions can be insufficient when joint distributions, nonlinear terms or interactions matter. No journal, regulator or registry mandates that PAIC-specific increment, and how often it is missing has not been quantified, though reviews of PAIC practice report inconsistent and incomplete covariate reporting (Truong et al. 2023, doi:10.1002/jrsm.1653). The nearest thing to a PAIC reporting standard governs the analyst’s own reporting of the comparison rather than what the comparator publication must carry (Ishak et al. 2025, doi:10.1007/s40273-025-01503-1).

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
Phillippo et al. 2019, International Journal of Technology Assessment in Health Care Review of 18 NICE technology appraisals using population adjustment; reports that covariates were included on the basis of availability, expert opinion, effective sample size, statistical significance or cross-validation, sourcing the availability-bounded adjustment set.
CONSORT 2025 statement, PLOS Medicine Trial reporting standard whose item 25 mandates a baseline demographic and clinical characteristics table per group; establishes that a mandated minimum baseline reporting set does exist, without specifying what population adjustment needs.
Harari et al. 2023, Research Synthesis Methods Network meta-interpolation: adjusts for effect modification using subgroup analyses reported in aggregate publications, with standard errors, instead of treating the reported covariate set as a fixed constraint.
Truong et al. 2023, Research Synthesis Methods Methodological review of PAIC practice documenting inconsistent reporting and incomplete covariate information; cited by the recency auditor as support that partial reporting is a live obstacle, without giving a prevalence estimate for the specific summaries PAIC needs.
Ishak et al. 2025, PharmacoEconomics Framework for reliable, transparent and reproducible PAICs; the closest existing thing to a PAIC reporting standard, but it governs the analyst’s reporting rather than the comparator publication’s covariate summaries.

Probable solution or research direction

Define and benchmark the PAIC-specific increment over CONSORT, namely which covariates, which dispersions, any joint or correlation structure, and reporting on the analysis set matching the reported effect estimate, and pursue it as an addition to trial reporting guidance rather than as an analyst-side workaround. Quantify how often that increment is missing, which no auditor found measured, so the case for a standard rests on evidence rather than assertion. In the meantime, methods that build effect-modifier adjustment out of the subgroup information aggregate publications do report already exist and should be extended (network meta-interpolation, Harari et al. 2023, doi:10.1002/jrsm.1608); treating unreported summaries as missing data propagates uncertainty only once identifying assumptions, external information or priors are added, so partial identification and sensitivity analysis are the honest fallback.

Verification

Verdict. Unverifiable. Auditors disagreed and the disagreement was evidence-bearing on both sides.

The source titled this. “No minimum covariate-reporting standard for aggregate-data trials” The title says no minimum covariate-reporting standard exists, but CONSORT 2025 item 25 already mandates a baseline characteristics table for each group.

The source said. “The covariate summaries that population adjustment needs are frequently absent from the comparator publication, or reported for a different analysis set, and no benchmarked minimum reporting set exists that would require them. Which covariates can be balanced is therefore decided by what a journal printed rather than by what causal reasoning requires.”

What was wrong with it. The claim was unattributed in the source and stated flatly that no benchmarked minimum reporting set exists; the literature auditor read CONSORT 2025 and found item 25 mandates baseline data (doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1004587), so only the narrower PAIC-specific version survives, and it is now sourced to Phillippo et al. 2019 (doi:10.1017/S0266462319000333). Dissent. literature dissented (overstated): This entry is source_unsourced, so I judged it against the literature. I read the CONSORT 2025 full text. It does contain a mandated baseline-covariate reporting item, item 25 ‘Baseline data’, and the statement describes itself as ‘a minimum set of items to be included in a report of a randomised trial’, endorsed by ICMJE, WAME and CSE, with the same item present as CONSORT 2010 item 15. Claim 2 as written (‘No benchmarked minimum reporting set of baseline covariate summaries currently exists’) is therefore contradicted at face value. Grok 4.5, recency lens
open / supported. Inverted search looked for a benchmarked minimum covariate-reporting set that would make aggregate-data publications PAIC-ready. Methodological reviews of PAIC practice document inconsistent reporting and incomplete covariate information as live obstacles (Serret-Larmande et al. 2023, J Clin Epidemiol; Truong et al. 2023, Res Synth Methods, doi:10.1002/jrsm.1653), supporting C2 and C4. CONSORT and trial reporting norms require baseline characteristic tables, but they do not mandate the full set of moments PAIC needs (means and SDs for continuous covariates on the analysis set of interest, consistent analysis populations, and joint/correlation structure). PRISMA-NMA update work (Veroniki et al. 2025 scoping/update papers) targets NMA reporting gaps (nodes, consistency, certainty of evidence) and is not a minimum AD covariate package for population adjustment. NICE DSU TSD 18 and EU HTA 2024 quantitative synthesis guidance describe what analysts need for MAIC/STC/ML-NMR but do not create a journal/registry mandate for publishers of comparator AD trials. No 2024–2026 standard was found that benchmarks a minimum reporting set and closes the reporting failure as a solved policy problem. C3 therefore still holds: the constraint remains what journals print. Treat incompletely reported AD as missing data with propagated uncertainty remains a proposed analyst-side response, not a solved standard.

How the evidence falls in time

21 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2012 to 2026. Every paper found here asserts the problem is open; none reports progress.

This problem is recurrent: three or more papers spanning at least eight years assert it independently. Sustained restatement by authors who mostly do not cite each other is the strongest evidence this reading can offer that a gap is real rather than one group’s framing.
