### The registered coverage band is derived from the wrong quantity, so the whole study's pass/fail is uncalibrated
SEVERITY: fatal
QUOTE: "The registered coverage band is 0.935 to 0.965, two-sided: an interval that is too wide fails it exactly as an interval that is too narrow does"
PROBLEM: The band 0.935–0.965 is asserted, not derived. It corresponds to nominal 0.95 ± 1.5 percentage points, but no derivation ties that half-width to any measured noise floor. Earlier in the document the floor for a *coverage shift worth claiming* is correctly solved from first principles (f=0.0791 from a 0.01 shift via 2Φ(1.96√(1-f))−1). The 0.005 MC error the study targets is also given. But nowhere is 0.015 (the band's half-width) shown to follow from the 0.004873 delivered MC error, or from the paired-contrast error, or from a multiple-comparison correction over the grid. If the band were derived from the MC error it would be roughly ±0.005 for a single cell and wider under the grid, not a flat ±0.015. This is exactly the "threshold asserted rather than derived from a measured noise floor" defect class, applied to the single most important number in the design.
WHY IT MATTERS: Every cell's verdict (too narrow / correct / too wide) is adjudicated against this band. If the band is too wide, methods that are genuinely biased pass; if too narrow, valid methods fail. Because the band is the study's pass/fail criterion and is *not* one of the numbers emitted by the generator (it is structural, picked by hand), it is precisely the kind of figure the no-typed-numbers guarantee does not protect.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: the band's half-width is derived elsewhere (e.g. 0.005 target MC error × a 3-σ grid-wise correction = 0.015) and the derivation simply isn't quoted in this document. In that case the finding is a missing-citation minor, not fatal.

### Prediction 1 is declared falsifiable by coverage approaching nominal, but the ladder is exempt from the gate—and the two statements contradict
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "The ladder holds every other factor at its middle and walks the target size, and the analysis reports it as the prediction-1 test" versus "The ladder is exempt from the gate, and prediction 1 is why."
PROBLEM: Prediction 1 says coverage of the superpopulation estimand does *not* approach nominal as nT grows. The document then holds the ladder below the gate because the gate *dropped exactly those cells*. But "reports it as the prediction-1 test" means the ladder's cells are the test, and "exempt from the gate" means they are the cells where the moment share is below 0.0791 — i.e. where the *power for a coverage shift* is below the 0.01 the floor was solved from. The ladder cells are by construction the least-powered cells in the design, and with 2000 replicates delivering 0.004873 MC error, a single ladder rung cannot resolve a shift below ~0.01. So the prediction is "tested" precisely where the measurement cannot see it. The sentence "A design that screens on the effect being large cannot test a claim that the effect persists when it is small" is true about the gate but does not rescue the ladder: the ladder is underpowered *for the same reason*, not for a different one.
WHY IT MATTERS: The central falsification path is declared to run through cells the study's own power calculation says it cannot resolve. If the deficit at the largest nT rung is, say, 0.006, the study cannot distinguish "prediction falsified" from "MC noise" and the falsification is indeterminate — the exact outcome the withdrawn second prediction succumbed to.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: the ladder rungs use more than 2000 replicates (not stated), or the prediction-1 test explicitly pools the ladder rungs into one contrast with adequate combined power (not stated, and section 7 says the ladder is retained *below* the floor, implying underpowered rungs).

### P5's B=800 selection uses mean width within 1% — a threshold asserted, not derived, and the table contradicts the claim that 800 is the smallest grid value meeting both conditions
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "800 is the smallest value in the grid meeting both."
PROBLEM: The stated two conditions are (a) paired coverage difference + its MC error inside ±0.01, and (b) mean width within 1% of the reference width. From the table: at B=400, paired coverage diff = −0.005 (inside 0.01 by itself), and width 1.006 vs reference 1.012 ⇒ within 0.006 of 1.012, which is 0.6% — inside the 1% criterion. The MC error on the difference at B=400 is not given, but the raw difference −0.005 is already half the 0.01 allowance, so unless the MC error exceeds 0.005 the *first* condition is also met at 400. The document asserts 400 fails without showing the MC-error term that makes it fail. Worse, the 1% width criterion is itself asserted: "mean width is within 1% of the reference width" appears with no derivation tying 1% to any consequence — it is the same "a criterion evaluated on a saturated quantity passes everything" flaw the document warns about, re-introduced as a guard against the saturation it caught the second time. The width condition does protect against the B=25-style over-wide interval, but "1%" is a magic number.
WHY IT MATTERS: B=800 is described as "the study's whole budget lever" and drives 99% of the 647.7 core-hour cost. If 400 actually meets both conditions, the cost is ~2× overstated; if the 1% bar is mis-set, either a cheaper B passes or no B in the grid passes and the true sufficient B is off the grid. Either way the registered B is not the smallest the document claims, and the "honest sizing" narrative that justifies the 531% cost rise depends on it.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: the MC error on the paired difference at B=400 is >0.005 (pushing the sum past 0.01), *and* the 1% width tolerance is derived from a stated downstream consequence that is simply not quoted in the protocol text. The document gives neither, so both must be shown.

### The cloglog/mixed quadrature validation reports "1.46 standard errors apart" but P1's own stop rule is "more than three"
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "on the cloglog/mixed cell at order 16 the quadrature gives 0.73049199 against a Monte Carlo estimate of 0.73038878 with standard error 7.08e-05, 1.46 standard errors apart. P1 stops and registers no order if they ever disagree by more than three."
PROBLEM: The measurement is presented as though it validates the rule (1.46 ≪ 3, so "the check RUNS inside P1"). But "disagree by more than three" is a guard that *cannot fail on the evidence shown*: a 1.46σ discrepancy is what you expect by chance on a healthy rule, and a 3σ discrepancy the document never approaches in any reported probe. More importantly, the comparison P1 makes is quadrature (deterministic, exact for smooth pieces) against *Monte Carlo* with se 7.08e-05 — so the Monte Carlo *is* the noisy reference. A "more than three" cutoff against a reference whose own standard error is 7e-05 means P1 will only refuse to register if the bias exceeds 2.1e-04, i.e. twice the registered quadrature tolerance of 0.0001. The guard's threshold is looser than the tolerance it is supposed to protect. The sentence "P1 stops and registers no order if they ever disagree by more than three" therefore describes a gate that, against this reference, would let through exactly the 3.03e-04 deviation that prompted the whole rewrite.
WHY IT MATTERS: The integrity of order-16 registration rests on a comparison whose own uncertainty swamps the tolerance, and the stop rule is calibrated to fail only at roughly 2× the tolerance. This is the "guard cited for a check that cannot fail" defect.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: the Monte Carlo reference standard error at the production seed count is much smaller than 7.08e-05 (e.g. the 7.08e-05 figure is from the probe's small MC budget and production uses ≥10×), so that 3σ is in fact below 1e-04. The document does not say so and quotes the 7.08e-05 figure as the relevant one.

### The P6 cross-term verdict conflates a ratio-share threshold with the same-named "floor" used by the gate, exactly the two-uses-of-one-word error the section claims to have corrected
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "The worst cell reaches 0.0962 including Monte Carlo error against the 0.0791 threshold, so the cross term is above the threshold and cannot be ignored."
PROBLEM: The 0.0791 floor was solved (section 2 / P2) as the omitted-variance *fraction of the whole contrast variance* that moves coverage by 0.01. The cross-term 0.0962 is presented "against the 0.0791 threshold" as if the same floor applies — but the cross term is a *covariance*, not an omitted variance, and its effect on coverage is not 1−f²-style. A covariance enters the interval variance as −2Cov(theta_AC, theta_BC), linearly, not as the quadratic form the floor was solved from. Reusing 0.0791 as the cross-term cutoff is the slide the section explicitly disclaims ("The comparison is therefore stated without that word") and then quietly performs anyway via the shared numeric threshold. The comparison needs its own coverage-shift solution, treating the cross term as a signed additive variance contribution, not a positive omitted fraction.
WHY IT MATTERS: "The cross term cannot be ignored" is registered as the *finding* that justifies the `maic_xcov` arm and the whole attribution decomposition. If 0.0791 is the wrong cutoff for a covariance, the cross term may be ignorable — precisely the opposite of the stated conclusion — and the decomposition loses its premise.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: the cross term is expressed as a fraction of total contrast variance and that fraction is in fact directly comparable to the omitted-variance fraction (i.e. both are dimensionless shares of the same denominator). The document says the gate denominator is "the whole variance of the anchored contrast" and the cross term is divided by the same — but it never shows the coverage-shift curve is the same shape for a signed covariance fraction as for an omitted variance, which is the step 0.0791 silently assumes.

### Section 7 revokes the ladder's purpose again, two sections after the section-2 cleanup claims to have fixed the earlier revocation
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "the growth ladder is retained below the floor precisely so that the persistence claim can be tested where the effect is small by construction. An earlier draft of this section revoked the ladder's purpose two sections after registering it."
PROBLEM: The document presents the *current* section 7 as the corrected version that does NOT revoke the ladder. But the sentence immediately preceding — "The study therefore says nothing about most conditions where the omitted variance is small, **with one deliberate exception**: the growth ladder is retained below the floor precisely so that the persistence claim can be tested" — is itself a restatement of the ladder's purpose as *testing* prediction 1. Section 2 already says the ladder "reports it as the prediction-1 test." So the *purpose* is now registered in two places identically; what changed is only that the revocation was removed. That is fine, but the framing "An earlier draft of this section revoked the ladder's purpose two sections after registering it" is a confession about an earlier draft placed where the correction itself should be, and it leaves open whether any *other* residual revocation survives. It also does not address the combined concern: the ladder is retained *because* it tests persistence, but is *below the floor* because the effect is small — and a small effect in an underpowered rung (2000 reps, se 0.004873) cannot be resolved as persistence vs. noise (see the ladder finding above). ### "maic_fixed, maic_entropy, maic_oracle and maic_xcov come off one fit and share a point estimate" — but each defines the estimand via a different correlation target, so sharing a fit cannot be true
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "`maic_fixed`, `maic_entropy`, `maic_oracle` and `maic_xcov` come off one fit and share a point estimate, so contrasts among them are comparisons of intervals and nothing else."
PROBLEM: `maic_oracle` supplies the target's *population* correlation; the others use the reported/borrowed/independence correlation. The weights produced by MAIC depend on the correlation that enters the moment-balancing (the entropy-arm variance adds a term in the assumed correlation; the oracle arms substitute a different one). Either the four arms use a single balancing solution and differ only in the *variance* they attribute to that same weighted estimate — in which case "share a point estimate" is true but "one fit" is loose phrasing for "one weighting," and `maic_oracle`'s correlation only reshapes the variance, which contradicts the oracle's stated purpose of isolating the correlation *component* as if it were a genuine alternative. Or the oracle's different correlation produces a different balancing solution (different weights) — in which case they do NOT share a point estimate and the paired-contrast-on-shared-estimate rationale collapses. The document asserts the former but the oracle description implies the latter.
WHY IT MATTERS: The "one fit, shared estimate" claim underwrites treating the four arms as pure interval comparisons with paired MC error from per-replicate differences. If `maic_oracle` actually reweights, its contrast with `maic_fixed` is a comparison of procedures (point estimate *and* interval), the paired-difference variance formula in section 6 misstates the contrast's MC error, and the decomposition the entropy arm "settled" in section 5 is confounded.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: MAIC's point estimate is invariant to the assumed correlation (weights solve a moment-balance independent of the correlation term, which enters only the asymptotic variance). The document does not state this invariance; without it, "one fit" is false.

### `maic_perturb` is excluded from the paired test for lacking a standard error, but percentiles have a standard error — the reasoning is wrong, even if the exclusion is defensible
SEVERITY: minor
QUOTE: "It resamples the source, refits per draw, and reports percentile limits, so it has no standard error and its interval is not centered on the shared estimate."
PROBLEM: A percentile interval's endpoints are order statistics and have well-defined standard errors (bootstrap quantile se ~ √(p(1-p))/(n·f(F⁻¹(p))). The claim "it has no standard error" is false. The real reason it belongs outside the paired contrast is that it estimates a different functional (a resampling target) and is not centered on a shared point estimate — which the sentence does say, but fronted with the incorrect "no standard error." A reviewer will latch onto the false clause and the correct one is buried.
WHY IT MATTERS: Minor for the verdict, but the false clause is the kind of sentence a critic will cite to discount the whole methods section, and it is the category the programme flags: a measurement (percentile intervals) used to support a claim ("no standard error") the measurement does not sustain. Correct to: "its interval is not centered on a shared point estimate and its target differs from the plug-in variance arm, so it is tested separately."
WOULD BE WRONG IF: "standard error" here means "the asymptotic sandwich-style SE the other arms report" as a narrow term of art. Even so the phrasing overstates; "no plug-in standard error" would be precise.

### The "first result... before any replicate was run" is presented as a finding but is the quantity the gate was calibrated to maximize
SEVERITY: minor
QUOTE: "That is the study's first result and it came out of the probe phase, before any replicate was run."
PROBLEM: The unanchored share (median 0.3612 on identity, etc.) is reported as the study's first result. The document reports shares with "min/median/max" across "the realized grid" (P2) and medians by link for retained cells (section 4); it is ambiguous which population the "first result" median draws from. If it is the full grid, this finding is wrong; clarify.
