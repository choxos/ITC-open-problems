### The cost figure is generated correctly but the sentence around it makes a false comparison
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "The cost rose by 546% against the 102.6 core-hours measured when B was typed at 200 and the grid was smaller."
PROBLEM: The 663.1 core-hours, B=800, grid, and 102.6 core-hours can all be emitted byte-identical from `registered-design.json` and still not support a "546%" causal claim. The sentence attributes the increase to two distinct changes conflated: (a) raising B from 200 to 800 (a fourfold multiplier on the perturbation arm only) and (b) the "corrected `mixed` arm added cells." The perturbation arm is stated elsewhere to be 96%-99% of total cost, so the B change alone — applied to ~97% of cost — accounts for the bulk of the 546%. The added cells are a second, unseparated contribution. Presenting them as a single attributable rise masks that the cell addition is an uncosted component of the delta, and the sentence cannot be right as a decomposition because it names two causes and quantities one.
WHY IT MATTERS: A pre-registration justifying a 546% cost escalation has to distinguish "we paid for honest B" from "we added cells." If the cell contribution is left inside the number, a reader (or the author, later) cannot judge whether honest B alone would have been acceptable, which is the only budget lever the design says it controls. The figure is correct; the claim about its composition is not measured.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: the generator stores the perturbation-arm cost and the non-perturbation cost separately and the 546% decomposes cleanly into a near-constant STC/MAIC term plus a B-driven perturbation term, making the two-cause attribution a fair summary rather than a conflation.

### The paired-coverage table is cited as measuring resampling bias while the shift is dominated by Monte Carlo error
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "At B = 50 the arm undercovers by more than three points from its own resampling budget alone, and the study would have reported that as a property of the method."
PROBLEM: The table at B=50 reports a paired coverage difference of -0.0325. The selection criterion is later stated as "the paired coverage difference from the reference, plus that difference's Monte Carlo error, is inside the 0.01 shift." The delivered Monte Carlo error at 2000 replicates is 0.004873 per cell. But this is a paired difference read from nested subsamples of one draw set; its Monte Carlo error is not the per-cell figure, and nothing in the document states what it is. The claim "more than three points from its own resampling budget alone" asserts the -0.0325 is attributable to resampling bias rather than to the Monte Carlo error on the 2000 replicates that observe the B=50 interval, yet no Monte Carlo error for the paired difference is reported in the table. The inward-bias mechanism is real, but the magnitude is asserted, not measured, given the error budget the study itself names.
WHY IT MATTERS: The entire selection of B=800 is the study's "whole budget lever." If the B=50 -0.0325 is not separable from Monte Carlo noise on the comparison itself, the grid in the table — 50, 100, 200, 400, 800 — is being read as a clean signal of resampling bias when it is partly a signal of the comparator's own noise. The selection rule explicitly says to include the difference's Monte Carlo error; the table omits it.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: the paired design (nested subsamples of one draw set) makes the paired-difference Monte Carlo error far smaller than 0.004873 — e.g., the same 2000 replicates share their draw seed across B values, so the dominant noise is the B-level quantile bias and the paired error is negligible.

### A threshold (0.01) is asserted as "the smallest coverage shift worth claiming" without derivation from a noise floor
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "The smallest coverage shift worth claiming is 0.01, which needs f = 0.0791."
PROBLEM: The document elsewhere distinguishes carefully between derived and asserted thresholds — revoking an earlier 0.04 floor precisely because it "was asserted to follow from the same sentence and does not." The 0.01 here is presented as "stated rather than implied" but the derivation only converts it to f; it does not derive the 0.01 itself. The sentence asserts the shift criterion rather than solving it from the noise budget that section 6 reports.
WHY IT MATTERS: 0.0791 drives the entire cell gate (356 of 792 cells run, 436 dropped) and the cross-term decision in P6. Its own basis is a typed 0.01 rather than, as the design claims for its other thresholds, a quantity solved from the measured error. The defect is exactly the class the document warns about: the number is correct, the sentence around it claims a derivation that did not happen.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: section 6's band derivation (expected spurious failures ≤ 0.1) implies the 0.01 shift as the corresponding per-cell resolution, so 0.01 is in fact derived from the same noise budget and the word "stated" is loose rather than substantive.

### The P6 cross-term conclusion uses a threshold solved for a different quantity
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "The worst cell reaches 0.0962 including Monte Carlo error against the 0.0791 threshold, so the cross term is above the threshold and cannot be ignored."
PROBLEM: The 0.0791 floor was solved in P2 from a coverage shift in the *omitted variance fraction*: "Omitting a fraction f of the variance reports a standard error of sqrt(1-f) times the truth." The cross term in P6 is `Cov(theta_AC, theta_BC)`, a covariance that can be positive or negative and is *added* (the sign of -2 Cov is opposite the sign of Cov). The coverage effect of dropping a signed covariance is not the coverage effect of dropping a positive variance share: if the cross term is negative, carrying it *widens* the interval (the -2Cov term is positive), and dropping it *narrows*, producing the opposite direction of coverage shift than the sqrt(1-f) formula gives. The threshold was derived for a one-sided omitted quantity and is applied to a two-sided one, so "above the threshold" does not mean "cannot be ignored" — a large-magnitude negative cross term has the same |f| but the opposite coverage consequence.
WHY IT MATTERS: "Carried rather than argued away" is the P6 conclusion and feeds directly into the `maic_xcov` arm and the attribution logic ("the difference from `maic_entropy` is the cross term"). If the threshold is the wrong shape for the quantity, the worst cell could be a large negative covariance whose inclusion actually *harms* coverage, and the design would still be endorsing it on the strength of a magnitude test.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: the cross term is carried in signed form in every cell regardless of magnitude (the 0.0791 check is only a "do we even need this arm" gate, not an inclusion criterion per cell), in which case the direction problem does not change what is implemented, only the gloss on the worst cell.

### The ladder's exemption from the gate is asserted rather than shown to need the exemption
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "THE LADDER IS EXEMPT FROM THE GATE, and prediction 1 is why. The prediction says the coverage deficit does not close as the target grows. The share falls as the target grows... so the gate drops the large-target cells first: exactly the ones the prediction is about."
PROBLEM: The argument is: the gate drops large-target cells, the prediction is about large-target cells, therefore exempt them. But the prediction is falsified *if coverage approaches nominal as nT grows* — which is detectable on a cell the gate retains (a middle-target cell with a large enough share to pass the gate and be powered), without exempting the underpowered large-target cells. Exempting underpowered cells from the gate means the ladder can report coverage on cells where a nominal-coverage interval would already be indistinguishable from a biased one within the cell's own Monte Carlo error. The exemption protects cells that the prediction says the deficit "persists" in, but persistence in a sub-powered cell is the one form of the prediction that the measurement cannot resolve. So the exemption is not "prediction 1 is why" — it is "we want to observe the prediction where it is hardest to observe," and the reason cuts against the design rather than for it.
WHY IT MATTERS: The ladder is the named "prediction-1 test." If its large-target rungs are below the floor the gate was built to enforce (i.e., the effect would be undetectable at the study's resolution), a "persistence observed" conclusion on those rungs would be a saturated-quantity pass of exactly the kind P5 revoked its B=25 selection for. The exemption reopens the failure P5 was careful to close.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: the ladder is reported *as* a growth curve with per-rung Monte Carlo error attached, so an underpowered rung is read as "no resolution at this size" rather than as "persistence confirmed" — in which case the exemption is a reporting choice and not a test.

### "The two settings differ by an order of magnitude in what the moment term is a share of" is not what the table shows
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "anchored is crossed with the whole core rather than varied around a middle, because the two settings differ by an order of magnitude in what the moment term is a share of."
PROBLEM: The median-share table shows, per link: identity unanchored 0.3612 vs anchored 0.0395 (9.1×), cloglog 0.1570 vs 0.0100 (15.7×), logit 0.0961 vs 0.0079 (12.2×). ### `maic_perturb` is excluded from the paired test for a reason that does not exclude it
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "It resamples the source, refits per draw, and reports percentile limits, so it has no standard error and its interval is not centered on the shared estimate."
PROBLEM: The document states elsewhere that "`maic_perturb` is not in that set and is not in the paired test. Against the others it is a comparison of procedures. It is reported in the same table and tested separately." The justification given (no shared point estimate, no standard error of its own) is a reason to not pool it into the *single shared-fit contrast*; it is not a reason to exclude it from any paired test. A percentile interval and an analytic interval on the same replicate can be contrasted per-replicate on coverage and width under common random numbers just as cleanly as two analytic methods — arguably more so, since both are computed on the same draw. The stated reason is true but does not entail the stated conclusion.
WHY IT MATTERS: The perturbation arm is 96-99% of the cost. Excluding it from the paired comparison means the design's most expensive arm contributes no observation to the central coverage claim (the band-against-nominal and the between-method paired tests). "Tested separately" leaves an underpowered standalone comparison, since paired Monte Carlo error is the design's whole precision argument. The cost is paid for a result that the analysis structure prevents from being used at full power.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: "the paired test" is a defined term of art in this design meaning specifically the four shared-fit methods (so exclusion is a definitional convention, not a statistical one), and the separate test of `maic_perturb` is itself paired per-replicate — in which case the sentence names a convention rather than making a statistical argument, and the practical consequence is absent.

### P1's Monte Carlo check fails its own stated stopping rule
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "on the cloglog/mixed cell at order 16 the quadrature gives 0.73049199 against a Monte Carlo estimate of 0.73038878 with standard error 7.08e-05, 1.46 standard errors apart. P1 stops and registers no order if they ever disagree by more than three."
PROBLEM: The check is sound (1.46 < 3). But the rule's justification is that "Two coarse rules can agree and both be wrong" — the earlier failure of order 48 vs 64. The replacement rule is "stop if the *independent* Monte Carlo differs by more than 3 SE from the quadrature." That is a necessary condition for trusting the quadrature, not a sufficient one: Monte Carlo at 7.08e-05 SE cannot resolve a deviation smaller than 3·7.08e-05 ≈ 2.1e-04, which is larger than the registered tolerance 1e-04 that order 48 failed. So the independent check the rule relies on is *coarser than the tolerance it is meant to protect*. The check cannot fail at the 1e-04 scale the design cares about; it can only fail at ≈2.1e-04. The rule "stops and registers no order if they ever disagree by more than three" therefore cannot detect the class of error (1e-04 level) that motivated withdrawing order 48.
WHY IT MATTERS: This is exactly finding category 3 (a guard cited for a check that cannot fail). The guard is real, runs, and is reported; it just does not run at the resolution that would catch a reoccurrence of the defect it was created to prevent. The registered tolerance 1e-04 and the stopping rule 3·7.08e-05 are inconsistent, and both numbers are emitted correctly while the sentence claims the rule validates the order.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: the Monte Carlo sample is increased at registration-time validation (e.g., SE driven well below 1e-04/3) and the 7.08e-05 is only the figure at order 16 in the reported check, with the registered stopping rule gated on an SE below 3.33e-05 — in which case the published SE is illustrative and the guard is adequate.

### "Median share of 0.824" of interval variance carried by the target trial's own effect — direction of cross term not stated
SEVERITY: minor
QUOTE: "the anchored contrast carries a cross term -2 Cov(theta_AC, theta_BC) that every published estimator drops" ... "In an anchored comparison the target trial's own effect carries a median 0.824 of the interval's variance"
PROBLEM: The interval variance is the sum of omitted (moment), source, target-trial, and cross terms (-2Cov). If the cross term is negative, its signed contribution *adds* to the interval variance (because of the -2), and the target-trial term's 0.824 share is computed *net of* that addition. The sentence reports the 0.824 but never states the sign of the cross term distribution, so a reader cannot tell whether the 0.824 is large because the target trial's effect genuinely dominates, or because the cross term is reducing the denominator. P6's worst cell 0.0962 is a *magnitude* ("reaching 0.0962"), not a sign, and the cross term is signed. Variance shares of negative components are ill-defined, and reporting 0.824 of "the interval's variance" without stating how the cross term entered that sum leaves the figure ambiguous.
WHY IT MATTERS: 0.824 is one of the study's headline results ("the target's baseline risk exists because otherwise the two estimands coincide"). If the cross term is materially negative, the 0.824 overstates the target trial's standalone contribution to the interval and the "anchored comparison is dominated by the target's own effect" reading is partly an arithmetic artifact of a negative covariance inflating the denominator's complement.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: "the interval's variance" is defined in the design as the sum of *magnitudes* of the components (so the cross term enters as |2Cov|), in which case the 0.824 is well-defined and the sign is irrelevant — but the document writes the cross term as `-2 Cov`, a signed quantity, suggesting the signed convention is intended, in which case the share arithmetic needs the sign stated.
