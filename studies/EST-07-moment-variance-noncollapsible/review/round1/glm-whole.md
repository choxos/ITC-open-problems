### The 0.04 "derived" floor is asserted, not derived from a measured noise floor
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "a coverage Monte Carlo SE of 0.005 makes 0.01 the smallest coverage shift worth claiming, which needs roughly a 0.04 variance share."
PROBLEM: The chain is presented as a derivation but the last step is a non-sequitur. A coverage SE of 0.005 supports a minimum-claimable *coverage* shift (~0.01–0.02). To translate that into a *variance share* floor you need the mapping between omitted-variance share and resulting coverage shift, which is precisely what the study is measuring and is not established a priori. The 0.04 figure is therefore assumed, not derived; an arbitrary omitted-share could produce a coverage shift anywhere on the curve depending on link, n_T, and alignment.
WHY IT MATTERS: The floor decides which of 288 cells are run and which are dropped. If the true share-to-coverage sensitivity is steep, cells at 0.03 are dropped despite producing detectable coverage shifts; if it is shallow, cells at 0.05 are run and waste budget returning "indistinguishable from zero." Either way the realized grid is shaped by an unmeasured quantity, and the downplayed/over-claimed cells bias the headline pattern. This is the sibling programme's category 3 verbatim.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: a probe or prior result is cited elsewhere giving the empirical coverage-shift-per-variance-share slope, making 0.04 a measured crossing of that slope with 0.01; no such quantity is quoted here.

### The logit "anti-conservative" verdict assumes a target it did not measure against
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "an analyst reading a logit MAIC would see intervals too narrow while one reading a Weibull MAIC would see them too wide."
PROBLEM: The variance ratio 1.324–1.438 compares the ported estimator's variance to something, but nothing in the table states the reference (true estimand variance? oracle? asymptotic?). "Anti-conservative" is a *coverage* claim, and coverage has not been measured — only a variance ratio has. A variance ratio >1 can still yield nominal coverage if the interval method is correspondingly conservative, or be anti-conservative in a Bayesian sense; the direction is not determined by variance alone. The probe measures variance, the sentence claims interval behavior.
WHY IT MATTERS: This is the document's lead practical consequence and it is stated as an asserted probe result (per the "results are asserted" rule). Overreaching from a variance ratio to "intervals too narrow" is exactly the class of a measurement supporting a claim about something it did not measure. The whole framing of "direction differs by link" as actionable rests on it.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: the reference for the ratio is the oracle-variance interval and the interval construction is the standard Wald/normal symmetric form, under which variance ratio direction does map to interval-width direction; that assumption is not stated.

### The identity-link "factor of 6.7 → within one MCSE of zero" is offered as an implementation check but cannot fail as described
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "A wrong implementation produces a gap that does not move."
PROBLEM: This is presented as the control that validates the curvature finding, but the falsification rule is unfalsifiable as stated. If the gap *did* shrink, that is taken as correct implementation; if it did not shrink, that is taken as wrong implementation. There is no condition under which a shrinking gap would be read as a bug, so the check cannot fail. Additionally "within one Monte Carlo standard error of zero" is a non-rejection, not evidence of correctness — failure to reject zero is the default outcome of low power, and the MCSE for the gap at n_S=8000 is not quoted.
WHY IT MATTERS: The curvature finding is foregrounded precisely because the identity case "behaves as it must"; if that control is unfalsifiable, the curvature contrast rests on an unstated assumption rather than a measurement. The weakest link in the evidentiary chain is the one asked to carry the most weight.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: a pre-registered threshold for the gap and its MCSE at n_S=8000 is specified such that "within one MCSE" has a quantitative criterion and a wrong-implementation signature is defined independently of the shrinkage.

### Gauss-Hermite order 48 is "forced" by a condition the design does not run

### Coverage MCSE of 0.005 is asserted, not derived from the replicate count
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "2000 replicates per cell, set so the coverage Monte Carlo SE at 0.95 is at most 0.005."
PROBLEM: The standard error of a binomial proportion at p=0.95, n=2000 is sqrt(0.95·0.05/2000) ≈ 0.00487, which rounds to 0.005. The sentence phrases 0.005 as the target that determined n, but the relation is exact and identity-like; 2000 replicates gives at most 0.005 at p=0.95 by construction, it did not need to be "set" to achieve it — any n near 1900 does. More importantly, the "at most" qualifier is wrong here: 0.00487 is the value at the *most favorable* p for variance (p=0.5 gives the largest SE; at p=0.95 the SE is smaller than at p=0.5). At p=0.5 the SE is 0.0112, so "at most 0.005" is false for the realized coverage range the cell actually observes; it holds only for the point 0.95.
WHY IT MATTERS: The entire replicate/cost argument and the 0.01 minimum-claimable-coverage-shift downstream both rest on this SE. If the observed coverage in a cell is 0.70 or 0.99, the MCSE differs, and the "minimum claimable shift" derived from it differs. The stated bound is direction-conditional in a way the document treats as uniform.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: the SE is understood as the worst-case-over-realized-coverage and a cell-level figure is quoted showing the SE bound holds across the coverage range the grid actually produces; as written it is anchored to the single point 0.95.

### Cost saving percentage is internally inconsistent with the two totals
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "Cost: 18.6 core-hours ... against 102.6 ... a 81.9% saving."
PROBLEM: 18.6 against 102.6 is a saving of (102.6−18.6)/102.6 = 0.8185, i.e. 81.85%, which rounds to 81.9% only if the underlying values carry more precision than the two decimal places shown. With the figures as displayed the percentage is carried to more significant digits than its inputs; a reader recomputing from the printed numbers gets 81.85% or 81.9% depending on rounding, and neither is exactly generated. Because the document insists every number is generator-asserted byte-identical, the displayed precision should be exactly what the generator emits; a mismatch suggests the generator rounds inconsistently or the inputs were truncated for display without re-deriving the ratio.
WHY IT MATTERS: This is the one place the "no typed numbers" guarantee can be tested by arithmetic, and it shows the percentage is *not* a deterministic function of the two displayed totals. If the generator emits 81.9% from un-truncated inputs, then the displayed 18.6 and 102.6 are display-truncated and not the values the percentage was computed from — which is a stale-display defect of exactly the kind §Provenance claims cannot occur.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: the generator emits the totals and the ratio from the same un-rounded internal values and only the renderer truncates for display, in which case the byte-identical assertion is against the rendered file and is fine; but then a verifier recomputing 81.9% from the *rendered* totals will fail, so the provenance claim still overstates.

### "MAIC matches one moment for a binary covariate, not two" is contradicted by the moment-count elsewhere
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "the `mixed` arm matches five moments where the others match six."  [vs] "MAIC matches one moment for a binary covariate, not two ... x² = x identically, so a balancing function that always forms two columns per covariate is rank deficient"
PROBLEM: The rule as stated (one moment for binary, two for continuous) implies the mixed arm's moment count should be the *sum over its covariates*; a mix of one binary and two continuous gives 1+2+2 = 5, and three continuous gives 2+2+2 = 6. But the rule "MAIC matches one moment for a binary covariate" is a general proposition stated as fact about MAIC, while the design shoots for six moments generally. If *any* arm has a binary covariate it must drop a moment; the §4 table does not list covariate composition per arm, so the reader cannot reconcile "five where others match six" with "one for binary." Additionally the cloglog/`mixed` arm is the one Gauss-Hermite order 48 is "forced" by (§3), and if `mixed` contains a binary covariate the order-48 justification in §3 conflicts with treating that covariate's mean as a quadrature integrand — a binary marginal is a two-point distribution, not a slow-GH-converging continuous one.
WHY IT MATTERS: This is category 2 (a claim operative in one section that another section uses differently). §3 relies on `mixed` being slow-converging-continuous; §5 relies on `mixed` containing a binary covariate for the rank-deficiency exception. Both cannot be the same `mixed` unless it mixes both, and that composition is not stated. The rank-deficiency exception and the quadrature-order justification each use the `mixed` arm to carry a different unstated property.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: the `mixed` arm is explicitly composed of (binary + continuous) covariates, the binary one drops a moment on MAIC, and the continuous ones drive the GH order; the composition is not stated in the document.

### The `maic_oracle` definition makes the "win" criterion for `maic_entropy` untestable from coverage alone
SEVERITY: minor
QUOTE: "If `maic_entropy` restores nominal coverage across the grid on the logit scale, the catalog's porting claim is supported"
PROBLEM: `maic_oracle` supplies "the true correlation" with fixed moments, so it isolates correlation uncertainty from moment uncertainty. `maic_entropy` addresses the variance estimator. The "win" sentence does not name the contrast that would support the porting claim.
WHY IT MATTERS: A coverage result here is real but would be a measurement of "MAIC-as-a-whole vs nominal," not of "the entropy port vs the variance problem the catalog names as solved." Category 1: the measurement supports a narrower claim about the *port* than the sentence asserts.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: the registered analysis explicitly contrasts `maic_entropy` against `maic_oracle` to decompose variance-port from correlation, and the "supported" judgment is conditional on that contrast; no such condition is in the win sentence.
