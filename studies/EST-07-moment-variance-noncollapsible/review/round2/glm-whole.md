### The 0.07913633 floor is asserted as "derived" but the chain that derives it does not yield the quotient it produces
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: a coverage Monte Carlo SE of 0.005 makes 0.01 the smallest coverage shift worth claiming, which needs roughly a 0.07913633 variance share.
PROBLEM: The derivation as stated is: coverage SE = 0.005 → smallest claimable coverage shift = 0.01 → required variance share = 0.07913633. The first two links are sane (2×SE for a detectable shift is a convention). The third has no visible derivation. A variance *share* (a ratio of two variance components) is not determined by a coverage Monte Carlo error, which is a property of the Bernoulli SE of the coverage estimator across replicates. The two live on different scales; the "needs roughly" is a bridge with no stated functional form, no reference quantity, and no measured noise floor. This is Category 3 (a threshold asserted rather than derived from a measured noise floor). Because the floor is the sole criterion that decides which of 288 cells are run versus dropped, and section 4 reports that 124/288 clear it, the number gates the entire realized design.
WHY IT MATTERS: If 0.07913633 is too small, the study runs cells whose "effect cannot be distinguished from zero at 2000 replicates" — the very thing section 4 says it is avoiding — and burns 2000×budget on null cells. If it is too large, cells that would answer the proposition are dropped and the coverage claim in section 5 rests on a thinned grid. Either way the headline "124 of 288" is load-bearing and inherits the defect.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: the document (or registered-design.json) contains the missing functional step mapping a coverage-detectability threshold into a between-component variance ratio, e.g. an explicit power calculation at 2000 replicates and σ_share under a specific variance decomposition. That step is not shown in the quoted text.

### The identity-link probe is offered as evidence about curvature but its measurement cannot separate curvature from implementation
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: The gap between estimator and estimand gradient falls by a factor of 6.7 across $n_S$ = 500 to 8000, reaching within one Monte Carlo standard error of zero... which is what makes the above evidence about curvature rather than about the implementation.
PROBLEM: The probe measures how the estimator-vs-estimand gradient gap scales with $n_S$ *under the identity link*, where section 3's own estimand construction collapses $\beta_{EM}$ and the two gradients coincide by construction. The argument is: "a wrong implementation produces a gap that does not move; ours moves by 6.7×; therefore the implementation is right and the remaining gap on curved links is curvature." But on the identity link the *expected* gap is zero analytically and the measured gap is pure Monte Carlo error; for it to "fall by a factor of 6.7 with n_S" is just the 1/√n decay of the simulation noise itself — any implementation, correct or wrong, that does not inject curvature on the identity link would do this. So the probe confirms the identity-link implementation is not adding curvature, which is a much weaker statement than "the implementation is correct," and it therefore does not discharge the claim that the curved-link gap is curvature.
WHY IT MATTERS: Category 1. The whole framing of section 2 — that the remaining small gap on logit/cloglog is a property of the link (curvature) rather than of the porting — leans on this probe, because without it the gap could equally be a porting defect that happens to be small. The conclusion in section 5 (the catalog is supported because the gap is "too small to break coverage") inherits the ambiguity.
WOULD BE WRONG IF: the probe's gap on the identity link is measured against an *analytic* zero with a Monte Carlo SE that is held constant across $n_S$ (rather than shrinking as √n), so that the 6.7× shrinkage is a signal above the noise floor. The text says "within one Monte Carlo standard error of zero," implying the SE shrinks too, which is the opposite of this condition.

### "Varies the shape" is presented as evidence the normal-reduction condition does not confine results, but it measures the wrong thing
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: The `mixed` and `lognormal` arms vary the shape; nothing here measures a covariate law outside those three.

So "we vary the shape" is being used to support "we have left the normal-law regime," which the measurement does not establish. The section effectively concedes this in the same sentence ("nothing here measures a covariate law outside those three") but the three are shapes, not laws.

WOULD BE WRONG IF: `mvnorm`, `lognormal`, and `mixed` name *joint laws* (not marginal shapes) and the Gauss-Hermite order for each is verified independently per arm; the section's "shape" is then shorthand. The text uses "covariate shape" and "covariate law" interchangeably, which is the defect, so the caveat would have to be located outside the quoted text.

SEVERITY: serious

### Summary

The dominant defect class is Category 3 (asserted thresholds): 0.005 coverage SE, 0.01 smallest claimable shift, 0.07913633 variance-share floor, and the "inside the across-replicate spread" criterion for B=800 are all thresholds quoted as derived, none with a measured noise floor or a stated functional link to the quantity they govern. The second class is Category 1 in the specific form the brief names — a correctly generated number surrounded by a sentence claiming a consequence (coverage, curvature, porting validity, normal-law-escape) the measurement did not make: "0.9886-1.074 → unlikely to break coverage," "6.7× shrinkage → evidence about curvature not implementation," "varied shape → not confined to normal law," "logit within 1-7% → supports the catalog." The third class is internal contradiction ("nothing is conservative on either scale" vs. cloglog 1.077-1.172). The brevity of the failing sentences — each is one clause longer than the measurement it quotes — is the pattern to fix: either shorten the claim to what was measured, or add the missing run that measures what is claimed.
