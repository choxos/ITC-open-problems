OVL-01 answers the refuting sentence of this entry. Its G-computation arm fitted natural-spline
modification, whose prediction variance grows where the source has few patients, and integrated it over
the target law including 27% of target mass outside the source's support. When the modification bent in
the unsupported region its interval covered 0.70 with a bias of -0.29 on a 0.04 truth, although it had
widened there: prediction variance rises with leverage, but no model-based interval represents
misspecification in a region the data do not reach. A diagnostic that multiplied unsupported distance by
the estimated interaction discriminated failed analyses better than weight-based ones (AUROC 0.59 to 0.66
against 0.48 to 0.52) and was still not usable alone.

Not answered: how a support diagnostic should be aggregated over a target distribution that is itself
reconstructed from published marginals (mean against tail functionals), and how the reconstruction error
propagates into that diagnostic. OVL-01 used a known target law.
