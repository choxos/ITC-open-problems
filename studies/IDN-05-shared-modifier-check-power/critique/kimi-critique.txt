## Fatal

**F1 — The verdict is impossible on the realized error scale, for any estimator, including a perfect check.** "Reported both against realized error and against the cell's systematic error" never says which one the 0.10 gate reads. Realized error includes estimation noise in d_A, d_C, which the true baseline does *not* remove: SE(lnOR) = 0.187 (IPD) / 0.209 (AgD) per study gives SE(d_C−d_A) = 0.12–0.21 → risk-scale SD 0.029–0.051 → **P(material error) = 0.31–0.56 at the global null, s=0**. A *perfect* check passes only null cells and posts 0.43 > 0.10 — it fails a verdict it deserves to pass; a check that never fires posts ~0.72. The realized verdict cannot rank a perfect check above a broken one. On the systematic scale the verdict is well-defined but deterministic (per-cell 0/1), and my forecast puts it at **0.63–0.67** — known before running. Fix: gate on excess error vs the oracle fit, or declare the gate reads on systematic error only.

**F2 — "The check" in the verdict is undefined: which rule, which covariate, what action on a fire.** Three rules (DIC-5, point-null posterior, margin-ε) × two covariates = ~6 readings with materially different answers. Worst case: if "fires" means *any* covariate, x2-misattribution firings (real: corr 0.25 leaks ≈0.25× the γ_A[x1] residual ≈ 0.03–0.10 against SE ≈ 0.17) remove high-error replicates from the conditioning set while relax-x2-only leaves the x1 error in place — **the verdict improves because the check fired uselessly**.

**F3 — Deployment weights unstated.** The verdict spans ~0.44–0.9 (realized) / 0–1 (systematic) under admissible weights. An unstated weight vector on the headline endpoint is tunable post hoc. (The "upper 95% bound" construction on a weighted pooled proportion also needs a stated method.)

## Serious

**S1 — The drift grid skips the decision boundary.** In units of your own ε=0.153, the grid is {0, 1.96, 3.92, 7.84}. No sub-material-nonzero cell, nothing in (0.5ε, 1.5ε) where a useful check must discriminate. Predicted power at drift=0.3 (=2ε, error already 2× material): DIC-5 **0.01–0.04**, posterior **0.07–0.32**. You will measure the floor and the ceiling and nothing between. Add drift 0.10 and 0.15–0.20.

**S2 — n=50 is a choice, not a constraint.** 6,400 fits ≈ 4.4 core-hours at your measured 2.5 s/fit; n=300 ≈ 27 core-hours on the 7 workers already budgeted. At n=50, per-cell SE=0.07 leaves the 8–16 cells with true power 0.02–0.10 (the entire spread-0.25 half at drift ≤ 0.6) unresolvable, and kills per-cell calibration. Only the pooled verdict survives — the least informative quantity in the design (F1).

**S3 — Priors unstated; M2 is prior-determined; the smoke-test 0.162 contradicts multinma defaults.** Under default `prior_reg = normal(scale=10)`, my per-cell contraction gap is **0.003–0.054** — 4–60× short of M2's 0.20. But your smoke test reports contraction 0.162 (SD_post = 0.84×prior), which under a SD-10 prior requires data information 0.004 (SE ≥ 15) — 50× weaker than my estimate (SE ≈ 0.6–0.7). Either the fits run an informative prior of SD ≈ 0.4–0.5 (then every posterior is prior-shaped and you're not measuring the check analysts actually run), or the 0.162 came from a just-identified split-both fit and doesn't represent M2's fits. State the priors; register the prior-free SD ratio (1.2–3.9×) alongside contraction; reconcile 0.162 before the run — M2's fate is decided there, not by data.

**S4 — `tau_re` is a DGM factor but the fits' `trt_effects` is unstated.** multinma default is `"fixed"`. If FE fits meet tau_re=0.15 cells, "power" there is confounded with heterogeneity misspecification — and it's the design's own arithmetic (RE SD 0.15 = 2× the ecological signal 0.075) that makes the confound first-order. If RE fits: state the DIC deviance convention, note τ from 6 studies is barely estimable, and re-measure cost/fit (2.5 s was a no-RE pilot).

**S5 — The error of the remedy is never scored.** See headline omission below.

**S6 — GOAL says "power and calibration"; the design contains no calibration analysis.** The verdict is one point on one curve. `response.md` gestures at a target-blind mapping with folds — put it in the contract or strike "calibration" from the goal.

## The most important thing it leaves out

**The error of the remedy — the study measures the check only on the branch where it does nothing.** The check is a decision rule: pass → share; fire → relax. You score the estimand only on the pass branch. But relaxing isn't free: γ_C's posterior SD is 0.16–0.73 log-odds (my information table), so the relaxed C–A contrast carries risk-scale error SD ≈ 0.03–0.15 → **P(material error) on the fire branch ≈ 0.45–0.85** — comparable to or worse than the pass branch at low drift, better only at high drift. Whether *following* the check beats always-sharing or always-relaxing is genuinely open, and it's the only question an analyst needs answered. As designed, the study can convict ("a pass doesn't guarantee safety") but can never rank the check against its alternatives — and per F2, the verdict can even improve when the check fires on the wrong covariate. Round 1's design had exactly this analysis (always-common / always-relax / check-then-relax, RMSE + decision reversal + net benefit) and the revision dropped it **while still paying for all four fits**. Scoring all four on the target estimand costs zero additional fits — only the `predict()` calls and bookkeeping are missing. That's the one-paragraph fix.

Runners-up: the (0, 2ε) drift band (S1); any confounded-ecology cell (δ_j correlated with baselines or δ_jk — the ecological route is unbiased by construction here, so this is a clean-room lower bound on failure); the margin rule's strategy profile (fires least, relaxes most conservatively — invisible without S5).

================================================================================
THE MOST IMPORTANT THING IT LEAVES OUT
================================================================================

The error of the remedy. The design measures the check only on the branch
where it does nothing.

The check is a decision rule: pass -> keep shared interactions; fire -> relax
them. The study scores the target estimand only in the first branch. But the
relaxed fit is not free: gamma_C is identified from 3-6 aggregate studies with
posterior SD 0.16-0.73 on the logit scale (my information table), so the
relaxed C-A contrast carries risk-scale error SD ~0.03-0.15, i.e. P(material
error) on the FIRE branch of roughly 0.45-0.85 — comparable to or worse than
the pass branch in low-drift cells, better only at high drift. Whether
following the check beats always-sharing or always-relaxing is genuinely open,
and it is the only question an analyst needs answered. As designed, the study
can convict ("a pass does not guarantee safety") but can never rank the check
against its alternatives — and, per F2, its verdict can even improve when the
check fires on the wrong covariate, because fired replicates exit the
conditioning set regardless of whether the remedy repairs the error.

This is especially conspicuous because round 1's design had exactly this
analysis — always-common vs always-independent vs check-then-relax, scored by
RMSE, decision reversal, and net benefit — and the revision dropped it while
keeping all four fitted models. Scoring all four fits on the target estimand
in every replicate costs zero additional fits: the fits exist; only the
predict() calls and the bookkeeping are missing. The verdict's whole framing
("does a pass earn reassurance?") is half of a decision problem whose other
half the design already pays for and then throws away.

Fix, one paragraph: in every replicate, score the C-A target estimand error
for all four fits; define check-then-relax as relaxing exactly the flagged
covariates; report P(material error), RMSE, and decision-reversal for
always-common, always-relax, and check-then-relax under the same (now to be
stated) deployment weights. Then the study answers both "is a pass safe?" and
"is using the check better than not using it?" — and the verdict's 0.10
threshold acquires a denominator it can be compared against: the material-error
rate of the strategy the analyst would otherwise have used.

Runner-up omissions: (i) the (0, 2 epsilon) drift band (S1) — without it
"calibration" is measured only at the asymptotes; (ii) any confounded-ecology
cell (study covariate means correlated with baselines or with delta_jk): the
ecological route is unbiased by construction here, so the study is a
clean-room lower bound on the check's failure rate, and real-world failures
will often arrive through exactly that door; (iii) the fired-branch cost of
the margin rule specifically (it fires least and relaxes most conservatively —
its strategy profile is invisible without S5's scoring).
