### Primary 3's E2 sign is reported with a magnitude the cited figure contradicts
SEVERITY: serious
QUOTE: "E2's is **negative**, so on the logit link over its eight confounded scenarios contraction moves the way an analyst would hope."
PROBLEM: The sentence frames E2's negative correlation as the *reassuring* direction ("moves the way an analyst would hope"), but earlier in the same section the document defines the reassuring reading for E1 as "low contraction is the reassuring value, so a POSITIVE correlation means the diagnostic becomes more reassuring as the answer gets worse." By that definition the diagnostic is reassuring when it gives low contraction (high prior-dependence) precisely *as coverage gets worse*. A negative correlation on E2 says contraction *rises* as coverage gets worse, i.e. the diagnostic reports *more* reassurance (higher contraction = more shrinkage = looks better-identified) as the answer gets worse — the same pathology E1 shows, approached via the opposite coefficient sign. The prose asserts the opposite sign carries the opposite verdict, but against the section's own sign convention both arms point the same way: the diagnostic is misleading in the direction of worse coverage. Under that reading the sentence is right and the section's sign convention paragraph is the mistake, not this sentence.
